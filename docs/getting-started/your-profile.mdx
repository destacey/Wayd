# Your Profile

After logging in, you can manage your profile and account settings.

## Viewing Your Profile

Click your avatar in the top-right corner of the application and choose **Account**. The **Account** page has five tabs: **Profile**, **Preferences**, **Sessions**, **PATs**, and **Claims**.

## Profile

The **Profile** tab shows your **First Name**, **Last Name**, and **Email**. They are read-only here.

## Changing Your Password

> Only available if you sign in with an email and password. If you sign in with an organizational account, such as Microsoft Entra ID, you manage your password through your organization's identity provider, and the button is not shown.

1. Navigate to your profile
2. Click **Change Password** at the top of the page
3. Enter your **Current Password**
4. Enter your **New Password** and repeat it in **Confirm New Password**. It must be at least 8 characters and contain an uppercase letter, a lowercase letter, and a digit.
5. Click **Change Password**

You are signed out afterwards; sign in again with your new password.

If this is your first login and you are asked to change your password before continuing, the same form opens before anything else. Choose **Sign Out** to leave without changing it.

## Preferences

The **Preferences** tab holds settings that apply to this browser only:

- **Remember column layouts** — saves each grid's column sizing, visibility, pinning, and order, and restores them on your next visit. Turning it off keeps the saved layouts; they apply again when you turn it back on.
- **Reset all grid layouts** — **Reset All** deletes every saved column layout on this device, and grids return to their default columns.

## Sessions

Each time you sign in — on a laptop, a phone, a second browser — Wayd records a **session**. Signing in somewhere new does not sign you out anywhere else.

The **Sessions** tab on your profile lists every device currently signed in to your account, and every application you have connected, showing:

- **Device or application** — the browser and operating system a device signed in from, or the name of a connected application
- **IP address** — where it signed in from
- **Last active** and **Signed in** — when it was last used, and when the session started

The session you are using is marked **This device**.

### Connected applications

An AI assistant you connect to Wayd's [hosted MCP endpoint](./mcp-server.mdx#connecting-with-sign-in) by signing in gets a session of its own, marked **Connected application**. The row shows the name the application gave itself and, beneath it, the site it belongs to. That site is what identifies it: any application can call itself anything. Connecting the same application on two computers gives you two rows. A change it makes is recorded as yours, and the record's [Activity](../user-guide/activity-log.mdx) names the site of the application it was made through.

To remove an application's access, choose **Disconnect** from the row's actions menu. It loses access to Wayd straight away; to use it again, connect it again.

### Signing out a device

Use the row's actions menu to sign out a single device, or **Sign out of all devices** to end every session at once. Sign out anything you do not recognise.

Signing out revokes a device's ability to stay signed in. That device can keep working for up to an hour, until the access token it already holds expires — so it is not instant. If you believe your account is compromised, change your password as well.

> **Sign out of all devices** disconnects every connected application too. Signing out does **not** revoke personal access tokens. Those are managed separately on the **PATs** tab.

## Personal Access Tokens

**Personal Access Tokens (PATs)** allow you to authenticate with the Wayd [API](../reference/api.mdx#personal-access-tokens-pats) without using your username and password. They are useful for scripts, automations, and integrations.

### Creating a Token

1. Navigate to your profile
2. Open the **PATs** tab
3. Click **Create New Token**
4. Enter a **Token Name** (to help you remember what it's used for)
5. Set an **Expiration Date** — required, one year from now by default, and at most two years from now
6. Click **Create**
7. **Copy the token immediately** with **Copy Token** — it will not be shown again

You can have up to 10 active tokens at once.

### Using a Token

Include the token in the `x-api-key` header of your API requests:
```
x-api-key: your-personal-access-token
```

See the [API Reference](../reference/api.mdx#personal-access-tokens-pats) for full details on API authentication.

### Managing Tokens

- The **PATs** tab lists your tokens with their **Status** (Active, Revoked, or Expired), when they expire, and when they were last used
- **Edit** an active token to change its name or expiration date
- **Revoke** a token when it's no longer needed — this immediately invalidates it
- **Delete** a token to remove it from the list permanently; expired and revoked tokens can be deleted to clean up

PATs inherit your permissions — they can do anything you can do in the API. Treat them like passwords and don't share them.

## Claims

The **Claims** tab lists the claims in your current sign-in, by **Type** and **Value** — among them the permissions your roles grant. It is mostly useful when working out with an administrator why you can or cannot do something.

Creating, renaming, changing the expiration of, revoking and deleting a token are recorded in your account's activity, which administrators can see. The record names the token, never its value.
