# Demand Management

Demand Management holds **what people are asking for before anyone has decided to do it**. A project in [PPM](../ppm/index.mdx) is a response already being shaped, and a work item in [Work Management](../work-management/index.mdx) is work a team has taken on. A request sits upstream of both: it is the ask itself, and it may never become either.

:::info[Feature flag]
Demand Management is gated by the **Demand Management** feature flag, which is disabled when first deployed. Until it is on, the menu section, its pages and its endpoints are absent.
:::

People submit, browse and follow requests. Each desk reviews its requests through a status workflow, agents move them through it and to the desk that should own them, and requesters see where their request stands and why.

## Key Concepts

### Intake requests

An **intake request**, shown as a **Request**, is one ask: a business need, customer feedback, an innovation idea, or a regulatory or contractual must-do. Every request has:

- **Number** — Sequential across the whole company, so moving a request to another desk never renumbers it
- **Title** and **description** — What is being asked for
- **Requester** — The employee the request is for, who may differ from the person who entered it
- **Desk** — Where it is reviewed. Every request is on exactly one desk
- **Status** — Where it stands in its desk's review (see [Statuses and the review workflow](#statuses-and-the-review-workflow))
- **Channel** — Where it came from
- **Expected benefits** — What gets better, and for whom, if the request is delivered. Optional
- **Need-by date** — Optional, and always with its basis (see below)
- **Assignee** — Optional. The member of the desk who owns the request: ownership, never an approver

:::warning[Titles are visible beyond the desk]
On most desks everyone who can view requests sees a request's title, even when its detail is kept to the desk. Write titles you would be comfortable with anyone in the company reading.
:::

### Intake desks

An **intake desk** is a review responsibility: what kind of ask it accepts, who works it, the process they follow and what they weigh requests on. A desk is not a team and not a department. One engineering team can serve two desks, and one person can work on several.

The people who work a desk are its **members**, each in one of two roles:

- **Agent** — Acts on the desk's requests: reads them in full, edits them, moves them through review, assigns them, moves them to other desks and manages their participants
- **Desk Admin** — Everything an agent does, plus runs the desk itself and is the only one who can delete its requests

Being named on a desk is all it takes: no permission is needed beside it, and working one desk gives you nothing on another. Someone whose employee record becomes inactive stops being a member of every desk.

No desk is set up on install: the Demand Management Admin creates the desks the company will staff, with their first members, and a company that runs no review desk has none. A requester always chooses the desk their request goes to, by the desk's description of what kind of ask it takes rather than by the name of the group behind it.

### Statuses and the review workflow

Each desk reviews its requests through a **status workflow**: the stages a request passes from submission to a decision. Every request starts in **Submitted**, and agents move it on from there. Wayd ships a default that every desk follows until it is given its own:

| Status | Category | Means |
|---|---|---|
| Submitted | Proposed | Received, and waiting for the desk to look at it |
| In Review | Proposed | Being reviewed by the desk |
| Waiting on Requester | Proposed | Waiting for the requester to answer a question |
| Accepted | Active | Accepted for delivery |
| Fulfilled | Done | Delivered |
| Declined | Removed | Not going ahead |
| Duplicate | Removed | Already asked for in another request |
| Withdrawn | Removed | Withdrawn by the requester |

A company shapes its own review — a single triage, staged assessment, a review board — by building workflows in [Settings → Status Workflows](../settings/status-workflows.mdx) and choosing one for each desk. What Wayd does with a status depends on its **category** and its well-known meaning, never its name, so the rules below hold whatever a desk calls its stages. An intake workflow must have at least one Proposed, Active, Done and Removed status, and statuses meaning Submitted, Waiting on Requester, Duplicate and Withdrawn; Accepted and Fulfilled are optional meanings. In Review and Declined carry no meaning, so a desk can rename, split or replace them freely:

- **Proposed** and **Active** requests are **open**: they count as waiting on their desk and can be moved to another desk.
- Who can **edit** follows the category too. While a request is Proposed, its requester and the desk's agents can edit it; once it is Active, only the desk's agents; once it is **Done** or **Removed**, nobody, until an agent moves it back to an open status.
- Moving a request into any **Removed** status other than Withdrawn — Declined, Duplicate, or a desk's own "Won't do" — needs a reason, which the requester reads on the request. So does finishing it in any **Done** status.
- A **Duplicate** names the request it gives way to.
- Only the requester reaches **Withdrawn**, by withdrawing their request.

### Channels

A **channel** records where a request came from: business stakeholder, customer feedback, support, and so on. A channel is not the desk, so a request raised by support can be reviewed on any desk.

- **One company list.** The Demand Management Admin keeps the list in [Settings → Demand Management → Channels](#managing-channels). Every desk draws from it, so requests can be compared by channel across desks.
- **Each desk offers the channels that apply to it.** A new desk offers every channel, including ones added later, until a Desk Admin chooses its channels in the desk's [Settings](#running-a-desk). A channel added to the list later is not offered by a desk that chose its own.
- **A desk can offer none.** Its submit form then does not ask where a request came from, and its requests have no channel, shown as **Not recorded**.
- **A request keeps its channel.** Moving it to another desk never changes or clears its channel, even when the new desk does not offer it, because it records where the ask came from. An agent can change it to one the new desk offers. A request also keeps a channel after the channel is archived.

### Tags

**Tags** are the reviewing team's own filing: the area a request concerns, the customer segment, the review cycle — whatever the intake team needs to slice requests by. They are neither the channel a request came through nor anything the requester picks.

- **Tags sit on axes.** An axis such as **Area** holds the tags you can choose along it. One company set of axes serves every desk, so a filter on *Area: Payments* means the same on every desk. Wayd starts with one axis, **Area**, which takes several tags and has none until you add them.
- **Each desk uses the axes that apply to it.** A new desk uses every axis, including ones added later, until a Desk Admin [chooses its axes](#choosing-the-tag-axes-a-desk-uses). Where desks need different values, they use different axes — *IT Area* and *Product Area* — rather than sharing one.
- **An axis takes one tag or several.** On an axis that takes several, tagging adds to what the request has. On one that takes a single tag, choosing another replaces it. This is set when the axis is created and cannot change.
- **Reviewers apply them.** An agent on the request's desk adds and removes tags, along the axes that desk uses. Everyone who can see the request sees its tags, including on a **Summary Only** desk, but the requester and participants cannot change them.
- **Deactivating keeps what is already tagged.** A deactivated tag, or every tag on a deactivated axis, can no longer be applied, but requests that carry it keep it, and it can still be removed from them.
- **Free tags sit on no axis.** On a desk that [allows free tags](#allowing-free-tags), agents can also give a request informal labels — *follow up in Q3*, *batch 7* — by typing them into **Free tags** in **Manage Tags**. A typed name that is already a tag on an axis the desk uses applies that tag instead. Free tags show after the tags on axes, never replace one, and are deleted once no request carries them. A free tag is listed in pickers and filters only to people who can see a request carrying it.

### Need-by date and its basis

A date on its own tells an agent nothing. Two requests can both say 31 December, one because a regulation sets the date and one because somebody picked it in a meeting. So a need-by date always carries its **basis**, the thing that makes it that date, and a reference saying what the date rests on.

| Basis | Family | What the reference records |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory | Fixed for us | The regulation or standard, and the clause |
| Contractual | Fixed for us | The contract, and the clause |
| External event | Fixed for us | The event, such as an end of support or a customer go-live |
| Commitment | We chose it | A note on the commitment, plus who made it and on what date |
| Requester preference | We chose it | A free note |

A *fixed* date moves only if the world moves. A *chosen* date has an owner, so it can be renegotiated rather than silently missed. Clearing the date clears its basis and reference with it.

## Using Demand Management

**Demand Management** sits next to PPM in the main menu, and holds:

- **Submit a Request** — Raise a new request. Shown to anyone whose account is linked to an employee record
- **My Requests** — The requests you asked for, and those you submitted on someone else's behalf
- **Requests** — Every request you can see: those on the desks you work, your own, and, if you can view requests, everyone else's that their desk lets you see
- **Desks** — Every desk, who works it, and what is waiting on the desks you work. Each desk is run from its own page

### Submitting a request

The first question is where the request goes, and it is asked only when more than one desk is taking requests: **What kind of request is this?** Each desk is a card worded as the kind of ask it takes, saying which desk reviews it and who works there. Picking one only decides who looks at it first; a reviewer can move it if it belongs somewhere else. When only one desk is open, the form shows which desk the request goes to and asks nothing about it. When none is open, the page says so and nothing can be submitted.

The rest of the form is the same for every desk, in three steps. From the first step, **Back** returns to the desk question, and the last step shows which desk the request goes to:

1. **The ask** — What you are asking for.
   - **Title** — The form reminds you that everyone who can view requests sees the title, even on a desk that keeps the detail to itself
   - **Describe it in your own words** — The ask in full
   - **Where did this come from?** — The [channel](#channels), from those the desk offers. Not asked when the desk offers none
   - **Requested by** — You, unless you are submitting it on someone else's behalf. Either way, both of you can follow it
2. **Why and when** — What reviewers weigh it on when capacity is short.
   - **What is it worth?** — Optional. What gets better, and for whom, if it is delivered
   - **Does it have to land by a date?** — Optional; **No particular date** clears it. Once you give a date, the form asks **What makes it that date?**, with the choices in two groups: *Fixed for us*, where something outside the company sets it (a regulation or standard, a contract, an event outside us), and *We chose it*, where a person here set it (a promise to someone, or when the requester would like it). Each choice asks the one follow-up it needs, such as which contract and clause, and for a promise, who made it and when. **Reviewers will see** previews the date as the desk will read it, and how it will be treated: a fixed date as immovable, a promise as movable by talking to whoever made it
3. **Check and submit** — The request as it will be sent. **Submit request** sends it and opens it.

**Continue** checks the step you are leaving; **Back** and the step names take you back without losing anything. Beside the form, **What happens next** lists the stages of the chosen desk's review workflow in order, each with the description its author wrote. Waiting on the requester and deferring are detours rather than steps, so they are mentioned beneath the path, along with the request being declined with a reason.

:::note
Submitting needs your account to be linked to an employee record, because every request names employees as its requester and submitter. If yours is not linked, the page says so; ask an administrator to link it.
:::

#### Through an AI assistant

An assistant connected to Wayd's [MCP server](../../getting-started/mcp-server.mdx), with the `intake` toolset, can raise a request for you: it picks the desk from the descriptions of the desks taking requests, asks where the request came from when the desk records a channel, and shows you the request before submitting it. It can also list your requests and tell you where one stands — its status, the desk's reason for declining or finishing it, and its status history. It can edit or withdraw your request too. If you work a desk, it can review requests with you — change their status, assign, tag and move them, record the projects they became, and manage participants — and a Desk Admin or the Demand Management Admin can have it set up desks, channels and tag axes. It sees only what you can see in Wayd and can do only what you can do here, so it never reads a desk's internal notes unless you work that desk.

### Finding requests

**Requests** is where reviewers work from, and where anyone checks whether an ask already exists before raising it. It opens on the requests still open — those in a proposed or active status, whatever their desk calls it; **Everything** adds the closed ones. Requests on a private desk you are not part of never appear, so every list, count and total on the page covers only what you can see.

The same filters apply to both layouts, so switching between them never changes what is in scope:

- **View** — A ready-made set of filters: **My desks** (open requests on the desks you work, offered only if you work one), **All open requests**, **Needs triage** (open requests nobody is assigned to), **Assigned to me**, **Ranked by score** (open requests on the desks you work, highest [score](#scoring-a-request) first, offered only if you work one) and **Fixed dates, open** (open requests whose date something outside the company sets). While the page shows open requests, each view shows how many it holds. Changing any filter afterwards leaves the view
- **Desk**, **Status** and **Channel** — Pick one or more. **Desks I work** stands for your own desks, so a shared link with it shows each reader their own; status names are matched across desks, so *In Review* means every desk's *In Review*. **Channel** lists archived channels too, and **Not recorded** for requests with no channel
- **Assignee** — Anyone, **Assigned to me**, or **Not assigned**. **Assigned to me**, as a filter and as a view, is offered only when your account is linked to an employee record
- **Need by** — Any date, **Fixed for us**, **We chose it**, or **No date given**
- **Tags** — Pick one or more, grouped by axis, to keep requests carrying any of them. Inactive tags are listed too, since requests keep them. Shown once the company has added tags
- **Order** — **Usual order**, or **Highest score first**, which ranks both layouts by each request's current score. Scores from different models are on different scales, so desks that score with different models rank in separate runs, one per model; desks sharing a model rank together. A score from a model its desk no longer uses ranks after the comparable ones, and requests with no score, or whose score you cannot read, come last

The filters and the layout are kept in the page's address, so a view can be bookmarked or sent to someone. If you work a desk, the page opens on **My desks**; otherwise it opens on every open request. Switch between **List** and **Board** with the icons at the right of the toolbar. **Reset filters**, the clear icon at the end of the filters, puts them all back to every open request. On a phone the filters fold behind a **Filters** button.

**List** shows each request's number, title, [score](#scoring-a-request) where you can read it, status, how long it has been in that status, its need-by date with what makes it that date, its age, assignee, desk, requester and channel, or **Not recorded** for a request with none, and its [tags](#tags), each named with its axis. **Submitted**, the date and time the request arrived, is hidden at first; turn it on from the grid's column chooser. Need-by dates are coloured by their basis — red for a date fixed for us, amber for a promise — and time in status and age turn amber after a week and red after three. Sorting **Need By** ascending puts the earliest date first, a fixed date before a chosen one on the same day, and requests with no date last; descending reverses that. Hovering a status shows its category. The desk links to its page, and the requester and assignee link to theirs if you can view employees; otherwise they are plain names.

**Board** shows the same requests as cards in columns, one per stage of the desk's review workflow, in the workflow's order, so a stage with nothing in it still shows. A heading names the desk the board is for. Desks that follow different workflows have no single board, so with more than one such desk in scope the page asks you to pick one. Each card shows the number, its score where you can read it, the title, need-by date, requester and time in its stage, with a red edge when the date is fixed for us; opening it opens the request. Moving a request between stages is done from the request, since some moves ask for a reason. On a phone the board shows one stage at a time, chosen from a list with each stage's count.

**My Requests** shows the list's columns, limited to requests you asked for or submitted.

### The request page

Opening a request shows its number and title, with its status and [tags](#tags) beside the title; clicking the status opens its history. An agent on the request's desk changes the tags with the edit icon beside them or **Manage Tags** in the actions menu, choosing from the axes the desk uses. A tag the request carries along an axis the desk does not use — one it brought from another desk — can be removed there but not added to. The trail beneath the title leads back to **Requests** through the desk the request is on, and beneath that a line says who raised it, when, and where it came from when that was recorded. The panel opened from the header lists its desk, channel, requester and assignee and, where you can read them, who submitted it and where it came from.

- **Details** — The reason for its current status, when it has one, then the description, the expected benefits and the need-by date, which says what makes it that date and whether it can move. On a desk that keeps its detail to itself, people outside it see a message saying the detail is limited to that desk, rather than empty fields
- **Discussion** — The request's conversation with its requester, and the desk's internal notes. Shown only to people who can read the request's detail; see [Discussing a request](#discussing-a-request)
- **Outcomes** — What the request became: the projects that deliver it. Shown to everyone who can see the request, as its status is. See [Outcomes](#outcomes)
- **People** — The requester, the submitter when it is someone else, and the request's participants. Participants can read the request and reply to it, but cannot change it, and being one does not assign them any work. Shown only to people who can read the request's detail
- **Score** — The request's current score, where it ranks among the open requests on its desk, the ratings behind it, and every earlier score. Shown only to people who can read the request's detail; see [Scoring a request](#scoring-a-request)
- **Status History** — Every status the request has been in, as a timeline or drawn on its desk's workflow. Without the request's detail you see each change without its reason or who made it
- **Activity** — The request's history, limited to what you can read

Anyone who can see a request can **follow** it with the eye at the top of the page, to keep it in their [Following](../following.mdx) list; the Following feature flag must be on. Following grants nothing: a follower reads exactly what the tiers below allow, and loses the request from their list the moment they can no longer see it — when it moves to a private desk they are not on, they are removed from a private desk's request, or it is deleted. Its history in their Following list shows only what the request's own Activity section shows them.

The bell tells a request's people when it changes, without their having to follow it: its requester, its assignee, its participants and anyone who entered it on the requester's behalf, as well as its followers. Each is told for their own reason and can mute that reason, and nobody is told about their own change. The requester hears when its status changes, when it is reassigned or moved to another desk, when someone replies in its conversation, and when its title, details, need-by date or channel change; an agent hears when a request is assigned to them or taken off them. A follower who sees only a request's summary is not told about changes to its details or participants. [Notifications](../notifications.mdx#who-is-told) lists who is told about each change.

**Edit** appears in the actions menu when you can change the request: the requester while it is proposed, or an agent on its desk while it is open. The desk cannot be changed here.

**Change Assignee** appears for an agent on the request's desk. The assignee owns the next move on the request; assigning someone does not make them its approver. Anyone who works the desk can be assigned, **Assign to me** fills in yourself when you work it, and clearing the field unassigns the request.

### Discussing a request

A request's **Discussion** section holds its conversation, so the questions and answers that get a request ready to decide stay with the request instead of in email. It mixes two kinds of message:

- **Messages** — Read by everyone who can read the request's detail: its requester, whoever submitted it, its participants, the desk's agents and, on an open desk, everyone who can view requests. Anyone who can read them can post one
- **Internal notes** — Read only by the desk's agents and Desk Admins and the Demand Management Admin. Only they can write one, and nobody else is offered the choice at all. A desk can [turn new notes off](#setting-how-a-desks-conversations-work)

The composer at the bottom starts as a **Message**. An agent switches it to **Internal note** with the control above it, and the whole box changes colour while it is one, so a note is never sent to a requester by mistake. A note shows a lock and **Internal note** wherever it appears. Enter starts a new line; **Ctrl+Enter** (**⌘+Enter** on a Mac) posts, in the composer, a thread's reply box and the edit box alike.

- **Threads** — A message can have a subject, and starts a thread. Replies to it collapse under it ("2 replies · last reply 3 hours ago") and open in place with their own reply box. Replies go one level deep, and a thread takes its first message's kind: everything under an internal note is internal.
- **Mentions** — Type **@** to mention someone, which tells them. On a desk that is not private, mentioning someone you could add as a participant yourself makes them one, so they can read the request, unless the desk has [turned that off](#setting-how-a-desks-conversations-work). A participant cannot add anyone by mentioning them. A mention in an internal note can name only the desk's members and adds nobody.
- **Joining in** — On a desk that is not private, posting on a request you can read makes you a participant, so you hear about it from then on. On a private desk, people are added only by an agent or, where the desk allows it, the requester.
- **Asking the requester a question** — An agent does this from **Move to**, choosing Waiting on Requester (the desk's own name for that status): the question they write there is posted here as a message, saved with the move, and the conversation shows the move on it. See [Changing a request's status](#changing-a-requests-status).
- **Changes in between** — By default the conversation shows the request's changes between the messages, as its Activity section shows them to you. **Messages only** hides them.
- **Order** — The conversation reads oldest first, with the composer at the bottom. The order button beside the heading switches to newest first, which puts the latest message at the top and the composer above it; Wayd remembers your choice in this browser. The heading also says how many threads and messages you can read.
- **Editing and deleting** — The **⋯** menu on a message, shown when you point at it, offers what you may do. You can edit your own messages; every earlier version is kept, and **edited** beside a message shows them. You can delete your own messages, and the desk's admins can delete anyone's. A deleted message leaves a line saying who deleted it and when; its text and earlier versions are removed.
- **Locking** — The desk's admins can lock a conversation, after which nothing new can be posted, replied or edited until they unlock it. A request's status never locks it: a closed request can still be asked about.

Each message notifies the request's people. [Notifications](../notifications.mdx#who-is-told) lists who is told about messages and internal notes.

### Scoring a request

A desk can rank its requests with one of the company's [scoring models](../settings/scoring-models.mdx). An agent on the desk opens the request's **Score** section and chooses **Score**, or **Re-score** once it has one, then rates each criterion of the desk's model — a level of its scale, or a number for a criterion with no scale. The request's need-by date and what makes it that date are shown beside the ratings, because urgency is rated by a person: no criterion reads the date. **Preview** shows what the ratings would score before saving.

- **Only while the request is open.** Scoring follows the same rule as editing: allowed while the request is proposed or active, and frozen once it is done or removed, until it is moved back to an open status.
- **Re-scoring keeps the history.** Each score is a snapshot of the model's name, every rating and every output at that moment, and saving adds one rather than changing the last. A request scored 1.6 in October and 3.3 in January, once a commitment to a customer was recorded, shows both, so the review can see what moved it.
- **Scores rank within a desk.** The Score section says where the request sits — "2nd of 7 open requests on this desk" — counting the open requests on its desk scored with the desk's current model. A score from a model the desk has since replaced, or from a desk the request was moved from, is kept but does not rank with the rest until it is re-scored.
- **A desk without a model does not score.** That is a normal setting, not a fault; the section says so.

The requester and participants read the score and its ratings on their own request, since a decision is easier to accept with the reasoning attached, but only the desk's agents set it. A score is not needed to accept a request.

### Changing a request's status

**Move to**, beside the actions menu, is offered to an agent on its desk. It lists the statuses of the desk's workflow, in the order the desk laid them out, apart from the one the request holds and Withdrawn. A status that needs nothing more moves the request at once; one that does opens a form asking for it:

- A **reason** — required for declining, marking a duplicate, and finishing a request that has no [outcome](#outcomes), and optional otherwise. The requester reads it on the request, so write it for them
- For **Duplicate**, the request this one gives way to — any other request you can see. One that is itself a duplicate is refused, so a duplicate always leads straight to the request that survives

A closed request can be moved back to an open status the same way, which also lets it be edited again.

Choosing Waiting on Requester always opens the form, which asks for a **Question for the Requester** in place of a reason. The question is posted in the request's [Discussion](#discussing-a-request), saved with the move, so the requester can answer it there. Leave it empty to move the request without asking anything.

#### Duplicates

Once a request is marked a duplicate, anyone who opens it and can see the surviving request is taken there, and that request says which duplicate brought them. The desk's agents stay on the duplicate, with a link to the surviving request, so they can see why and reopen it.

Someone who cannot see the surviving request stays on the duplicate. They see it marked Duplicate, with your reason, and nothing about the other request. Whether the requester can see it depends on that request's desk, as [Who Can See a Request](#who-can-see-a-request) describes: a request on a private desk they are not part of is hidden from them, and so is one on any other desk if they cannot view requests. A request that others are marked duplicates of cannot be deleted.

### Outcomes

Once a request is accepted, its desk records what it became, so the requester can follow their ask from the decision into delivery. Each outcome points at a project:

- **New project** — A project started to deliver the request
- **Existing project scope** — A project that already existed and took the request on. Its note, which is required, says what the project absorbed; recording it does not change the project's own definition

**Add Outcome**, in the request's **Outcomes** section, is offered to an agent on the request's desk while the request is in an active status, such as Accepted. It lists the projects you can see in PPM, apart from those the request already points at, so a request points at a project once. Outcomes can be added and removed only while the request is accepted; once it is finished or closed they are part of its record, and moving it back to an active status opens them again. Removing an outcome never changes the project.

A request with an outcome can be finished without a reason, since the outcome says what delivered it. Finishing one with no outcome still needs a reason. If a project is later deleted, its outcome stays on the request, marked as a project that has been deleted.

A project created moments ago is offered straight away, but adding it may be refused as not found for a short while; if so, try again shortly.

#### From the project

With Demand Management on, a project's page has a **Requests** section listing the requests it came from or absorbed, with each request's status and what the project took on. Each request is listed only to people who can see it, by its desk's rules: a request on a private desk you are not part of does not appear, and nothing says it is there.

### Withdrawing a request

**Withdraw Request** is offered to a request's requester while it is open. Withdrawing tells the desk you no longer need it; you can leave a note for the desk saying why. The request stays on record, marked Withdrawn, and an agent can reopen it. Whoever submitted a request on someone's behalf cannot withdraw it — it is the requester's ask.

**Add Participant** appears when you can give someone access: an agent on the request's desk, or its requester or submitter unless the desk is private and does not allow it. Only an agent on the desk can remove a participant, and removing one takes away their access from then on; what they have already read stays read.

Deleting a request is a Desk Admin's last resort, and the request page does not offer it yet; it is available through the API. It is allowed only while the request has never left the status it was submitted in and no other request is marked a duplicate of it. Once a request has moved through review it is part of the record of a decision: decline it instead, and the requester can withdraw it.

### Moving a request to another desk

A requester picks the desk they think fits, and an agent confirms it or moves the request to the desk that should own the decision. **Move to Another Desk** in the request's actions menu is offered to an agent on the desk the request is on now. The new desk does not have to be one you work: it can be any desk open to new requests, including one requesters cannot choose. Nothing moves a request automatically — editing its channel, for example, never changes its desk. Moving a request never changes its channel either, even when the new desk does not offer it.

When you choose a desk, the form asks what the request **arrives as** there: one of that desk's open statuses that needs no reason and is neither Withdrawn nor Duplicate, since what to decide about it is the new desk's call. It starts at the status the request holds when both desks follow the same workflow, or the one with the same meaning or name when they do not; when nothing matches, you choose. A move onto a desk with a different workflow is recorded in the request's status history as a switch onto that workflow.

The form also says what the move changes:

- **A less private desk.** Moving from Private to Summary Only, or from either to Open, publishes words the requester wrote expecting them to stay with the first desk. The form names what becomes visible to everyone who can view requests, and the move goes ahead only once you confirm it. A moved request is read by its new desk's rules from start to finish, so its history comes with it:
  - Reaching **Summary Only** from Private exposes the title, requester, channel, need-by date and assignee — with earlier titles, past assignees and every desk the request has been on, including the private one.
  - Reaching **Open** from Summary Only exposes the description, expected benefits and participants, who submitted it and where it came from, earlier versions of the description, and who made each change in its history.
  - Reaching **Open** from Private exposes both.
- **A desk the assignee does not work.** The request's assignee is always a member of its desk, so moving it to a desk they are not on unassigns it.

A move to an equally or more private desk needs no confirmation. Every move is recorded in the request's activity with both desks, and the requester always sees which desk has their request. If you move a request to a private desk you do not work, you will no longer be able to open it.

### Desks

**Desks** lists every desk with its key, linking to the desk's page, its visibility, how many members it has and whether it is open to new requests. On the desks you work, it also shows what is waiting there: how many open requests, and how long ago the oldest of them was submitted. Nobody else sees those numbers — a private desk's requests are never counted for anyone outside it.

:::warning[A desk with no members]
A desk nobody works is flagged **No members** wherever desks are listed, and its page says so above its requests. Requests sent there have nobody looking at them, so name its agents or close it.
:::

#### Setting up a desk

The Demand Management Admin creates a desk from **Create Desk** on the **Desks** page, giving it:

- A **name**, which is what its members and administrators call it
- **What to bring here**, the description requesters choose by. Describe the kind of ask it takes rather than the group behind it
- Its **visibility** — see [Who Can See a Request](#who-can-see-a-request)
- Whether **requesters can choose it**, and whether it is **open to new requests**
- On a private desk, whether **requesters may add participants**
- Its **Desk Admins** and **Agents**. Someone holds one role on a desk, and a desk with no members has nobody to look at what arrives

#### Running a desk

A desk is run from its own page, opened from **Desks** or from the desk named on any request. The page has three sections:

- **Requests** — The requests on the desk that you can see
- **Members** — Who works the desk, and in which role
- **Settings** — Its name, what to bring here, its review workflow, who can see its requests (with what that exposes, in plain words), the channels and tag axes it offers, how its conversations work, whether it is open, and what requesters may do on it

What you can change depends on your role:

| Change | Desk Admin | Demand Management Admin |
|---|---|---|
| Name and what to bring here | Yes | Yes |
| Open to new requests, requesters can choose it, requesters may add participants | Yes | Yes |
| **Channels offered** | Yes | Yes |
| **Tag axes offered** | Yes | Yes |
| **Conversations**: internal notes, mentions | Yes | Yes |
| Name or remove **Agents** | Yes | Yes |
| **Review workflow** | Yes, while no request is on it | Yes, while no request is on it |
| Name or remove **Desk Admins** | No | Yes |
| **Visibility** | No | Yes |
| **Delete** the desk | No | Yes, while no request is on it |

Removing someone from a desk unassigns them from the requests they own there.

#### Choosing the channels a desk offers

**Channels offered** in the desk's Settings is either **Every channel, including ones added later** — what a new desk starts with — or **Only the channels chosen here**, with the channels ticked. Ticking none means the desk offers no channel: its submit form does not ask where a request came from. An archived channel stays ticked on a desk that chose it, but no desk offers it until it is restored, and it cannot be newly ticked.

Changing what a desk offers changes only what new requests and edits can choose. Requests already on the desk keep their channels, and editing one offers the desk's channels plus the one it has.

#### Choosing the tag axes a desk uses

**Tag axes offered** in the desk's Settings is either **Every tag axis, including ones added later** — what a new desk starts with — or **Only the tag axes chosen here**, with the axes ticked. Ticking none means the desk's requests are not tagged. A deactivated axis stays ticked on a desk that chose it, but no desk uses it until it is activated, and it cannot be newly ticked.

Changing what a desk uses changes only what its agents can apply. Requests keep every tag they already have, and anyone who can see a request still sees all its tags; the **Tags** filter on **Requests** still lists every axis.

#### Allowing free tags

**Allow free tags** in the desk's Settings lets the desk's agents add [free tags](#tags) — tags on no axis, typed in while tagging. It is off for a new desk, so a desk's filing stays on the company axes unless its Desk Admin chooses otherwise. Turning it off stops new free tags being added on the desk; requests keep the ones they carry, and they can still be removed.

#### Setting how a desk's conversations work

**Conversations** in the desk's Settings says how [discussions](#discussing-a-request) on its requests behave, and **Change Conversation Settings** lets a Desk Admin or the Demand Management Admin change it:

- **Internal notes** — On for a new desk. Turning them off stops anyone posting a new note or replying in a note's thread. Notes already written stay, readable only by the people who could read them before, and their authors can still edit or delete them.
- **Mentions give access** — On for a new desk. While on, mentioning someone you could add as a participant yourself makes them one. Turned off, only the request's own people and the desk's members can be mentioned, and nobody else is offered — useful on a desk that takes requests from customers, where access should be granted on purpose.

A desk that has not changed these follows the default for every desk, and the panel says so. Setting them back to the default's values makes the desk follow the default again, including any later change to it. Each change is recorded in the Activity log with what it was before. Turning a setting off never shows anyone something they could not already read.

#### Choosing a desk's scoring model

**Scoring Model** in the desk's Settings names the model the desk's agents [score its requests](#scoring-a-request) with, or **None**. **Change Scoring Model** lets a Desk Admin or the Demand Management Admin choose any active model, or clear it to stop scoring. Models are built in [Settings → Scoring Models](../settings/scoring-models.mdx), which needs the scoring model permissions; choosing one for a desk does not.

Changing the model changes only what the next score uses. Scores already recorded keep the model they were scored with, and only scores from the desk's current model rank together. A model archived after it was chosen stays on the desk, with a warning on the request's Score section, until a Desk Admin chooses another.

#### Choosing a desk's review workflow

**Review Workflow** in the desk's Settings names the workflow its requests follow, marked **Organization default** while the desk follows the default. **Change Workflow** lets a Desk Admin or the Demand Management Admin choose any published intake workflow. The workflows themselves are built and changed in [Settings → Status Workflows](../settings/status-workflows.mdx), which needs the status workflow permissions.

Choosing a workflow other than the default gives the desk its own, so a later change to the default no longer reaches it. Choosing the default's workflow again puts the desk back on the default, and changes to it reach the desk once more. A desk's workflow can be chosen only while no request is on it, closed ones included — moving a desk's requests onto another workflow is not supported yet. Choose it when you set the desk up, before requesters can pick it.

If someone else changes the desk while you have its settings or members open, your save is refused with a message to reload, rather than quietly undoing their change — and saving again from the same form is refused too. Close the form, reopen it to see their change, and make yours again.

A desk with requests on it cannot be deleted, because every request is always on exactly one desk. To retire one, **close** it to new requests and turn off **Requesters can choose it**; the requests already there stay put.

### Managing channels

The Demand Management Admin keeps the company's list of channels in **Settings → Demand Management → Channels**:

- **Add Channel** adds one to the end of the list. Desks offering every channel offer it at once; desks that chose their channels offer it once a Desk Admin ticks it.
- **Edit** renames a channel or changes its description. Every request from it shows the new name.
- **Drag a row by its grab handle** to reorder the list, which is the order requesters see.
- **Archive** takes a channel out of use: no desk offers it and no new request can be given it, while requests already from it keep it. **Restore** brings it back. Channels are archived rather than deleted, because requests refer to them.

Every change is recorded in the activity log.

### Managing tag axes

The Demand Management Admin keeps the [tag](#tags) axes in **Settings → Demand Management → Tag Axes**:

- **Add an axis** with a name, a description, and whether a request can carry several of its tags. That last choice cannot be changed later.
- **Open an axis** to add, rename, deactivate or delete its tags. Each tag shows how many requests carry it, counting only requests you can see.
- **Drag a row by its grab handle** to reorder the axes, which is the order a request's tags are listed in.
- **Deactivate** an axis or a tag to stop it being applied; requests that carry it keep it. **Activate** puts it back.
- **Delete** removes an axis or a tag that no request carries. Once a request has it, deactivate it instead.

The **Free tags** section of the same page lists the free tags agents have added, with how many requests carry each:

- **Edit** changes a free tag's name or description. Names are unique among free tags, ignoring case.
- **Merge into…** moves every request carrying a free tag, on every desk, to another tag and deletes the free tag — into another free tag to fold near-duplicates together, or into a tag on an axis to promote it: add the tag to its axis first, then merge into it.
- **Delete** takes a free tag off every request carrying it and deletes it, without having to be unused first.

Every change is recorded in the activity log.

## Who Can See a Request

A request is read in three tiers:

- **Summary** — Number, title, desk, status, requester, channel, assignee, tags, and the need-by date with its basis and reference
- **Substance** — Description, expected benefits, who submitted it, its participants, where it came from, the reason for its status, evidence, replies, score and ratings
- **Internal** — Internal notes and triage reasoning, for the desk's members only

Each desk sets how much of that reaches the rest of the company:

| Desk visibility | Everyone who can view requests | The desk's members and the request's own people |
|---|---|---|
| **Open** | Summary and substance | Everything their role allows |
| **Summary only** | Summary | Everything their role allows |
| **Private** | Nothing. The request appears in no list, search or count | Everything their role allows |

A request's own people are its **requester**, whoever **submitted** it on the requester's behalf, and any **participants** given access to it. Participants read the request and can join the discussion, but they cannot change it and never see internal notes. On a desk that is not private, someone becomes a participant by replying, or by being mentioned by someone who could have added them.

Working one desk gives you nothing on another: an agent reads other desks' requests only as far as those desks let everyone. Someone who cannot view requests sees only their own requests and those on the desks they work.

A request's history shows only the changes to what you can read. If you can read only the summary, the history and the status history also leave out who made each change, because whoever submitted a request on someone's behalf is part of its substance, and the status history leaves out each change's reason.

A duplicate shows the request it gives way to only to readers who can see that request. Anyone else sees it marked Duplicate and nothing about the other request.

Request numbers run across the whole company, so a gap in the numbers you see can mean requests you cannot see: on a private desk, or deleted. The number never says what they are.

## Permissions

Most of what happens to a request is decided by [desk roles](#intake-desks), not permissions, so Demand Management has only two:

| Permission | What it allows |
|---|---|
| View | Browse other people's requests, within the desk visibility above |
| Administer | The Demand Management Admin: create and delete desks, change a desk's visibility, name Desk Admins, manage channels and tag axes, and act as Desk Admin on every desk |

Submitting a request, and reading, editing (while it is proposed), withdrawing and replying to your own, need no permission. See [Demand Management: desk roles](../settings/permissions.mdx#demand-management-desk-roles).
