# Following

Following a record keeps it in your **Following** list, so the projects, programs and planning interval objectives you care about are in one place. Following is behind the **Following** feature flag; an administrator turns it on in **Settings → Feature Flags**.

:::note
When something changes on a record you follow — its status, its dates, a new health check — Wayd records an in-app notification for you. The notification bell is not in the app yet; until it is, an assistant connected to Wayd's [hosted MCP endpoint](../getting-started/mcp-server.mdx) can list them for you. Notifications are never sent by email, Teams or push.
:::

## Follow a record

Open a project, program or PI objective and select the **Follow** button (the eye) at the top of the page. The eye fills in while you follow the record; select it again to stop following. Hover over it to see which it is.

You can follow:

| Record | Read permission it needs |
|--------|--------------------------|
| [Projects](./ppm/projects.mdx) | View Projects |
| [Programs](./ppm/portfolios-programs.mdx) | View Programs |
| [PI Objectives](./planning/planning-intervals.mdx) | View Planning Interval Objectives |

Following a record you already follow changes nothing, and stopping following one you do not follow is not an error.

## Your Following list

Open your profile menu (top right) and select **Following**. The page lists everything you follow together, most recently changed first. Each row shows the record's health, its kind (Project, Program or PI Objective), its key, an objective's planning interval, when it last changed and its status. "Last changed" counts everything the record's own Activity section lists.

- **Narrow the list** by kind of record — Projects, Programs, PI objectives — or to what needs a look. On a wide screen the side rail picks one or the other; on a narrower one the filter row above the list combines a kind with a view. The views are **Health is red** (an unhealthy current health check), **Changed this week** (any activity in the last seven days) and **Closed or done** (completed, canceled or missed — the records worth pruning). Each shows how many it holds. **Filter** by name, or by key exactly, and **sort** by when records last changed or when you followed them; the arrow beside the sort switches between newest and oldest first. Sorted oldest first by last change, records with no activity come first: the ones most likely to have gone quiet.
- **Select a record** to see it beside the list: its status and health, a project's stages or an objective's progress, its owners and managers (an objective's team), its dates, when you started following it, and what has happened to it since. Select the record's name to open it, or the eye at the top right to stop following it; the next record in the list takes its place. Drag the bar between the list and the record to give either more room; the page remembers where you left it.

On a phone, the list is the page: tap a record to open its details, with **Open** and **Unfollow** pinned at the bottom, and use **Back to the list** (or your browser's back) to return to where you were. With fewer than ten records followed, the page shows just the list and the selected record.

## What following does not do

- **It grants nothing.** You can follow only a record you can already see, and following it never lets you see more. If you lose access to a record — a permission is removed, or the record is deleted — it disappears from your list and from its group's count.
- **It is only for yourself.** Nobody can follow a record on your behalf, and you cannot add someone else as a follower.
- **It needs an employee.** A follow belongs to the employee your account is linked to. An account with no linked employee does not see the Follow button.

Following is different from being part of a record. Owners, managers and members are given a record and the access that goes with it; a follower chose a record they could already see, and gains nothing by following it.

## Notifications

You are told about a change to a record when you follow it, and also when you take part in it: you hold a role on a project or program, or you are on the team that owns a PI objective. If both are true you are told once, with both reasons. You are never told about your own changes, or about changes made by an import.

Notifications follow the same rule as your list: you see one only while you can still see its record. Notifications are kept for 90 days.
