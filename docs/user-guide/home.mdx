# Home

The home page is a briefing on your work: what needs your attention today, what has changed on the records you follow or work on, and the work items, projects, tasks, deliverables, risks, planning intervals, teams, intake desks and requests you are part of.

It opens with a greeting and today's date, then a row of counts across the top, then the widgets in two columns: a wider main column and a narrower side column. On a tablet the side column's widgets sit two abreast below the main column; on a phone everything is one column, with the side column's widgets last.

The page appears straight away. Each widget loads on its own and shows a placeholder until its data arrives, so a slow widget never holds up the rest.

## What you see

You see only the widgets that apply to you. A widget needs some or all of:

- **A permission.** My projects needs permission to view projects, and My work items permission to view work items, for example. Without it the widget is not shown.
- **A feature flag.** What's changed and Following need the **Following** feature flag, and Desk queue and My requests the **Demand Management** flag, which an administrator turns on in **Settings → System → Feature Flags**.
- **An account linked to an employee.** Anything about "my" work — work items, projects, risks, tasks, teams, notifications, the records you follow — comes from the employee record your account is linked to.
- **A duty only some people hold.** My project tasks shows only once you have a task assigned on an approved or active project. Project health checks due shows only if you are an Owner or Manager on an approved or active project, since reporting a project's health falls to them; being a sponsor, member or task assignee, or leading the project's program or portfolio, does not add it. My roadmaps shows only if you are an owner of an active roadmap. Each appears once you take that on.

If your account is not linked to an employee, a banner at the top says so. Your work items, projects, risks, tasks, teams, notifications and the records you follow appear once an administrator links your account in **Settings → Access → Users**.

Every widget is a card with its title at the top left and, where there is a full list behind it, **View all** at the top right. A widget that applies to you but has nothing to show says, in one line, how it fills. Its [When there is nothing to show](#widget-settings) setting can shorten that to its title alone, or hide it until it has something.

## Needs attention

The row of counts across the top shows up to four numbers that need you today. Each one links to the list behind it, and its number turns orange or red. A count at zero gives up its place to the next one, and when every count is zero the row is not shown at all, so an empty row means nothing is waiting.

When more counts are above zero than fit, the most urgent four show, in this order: overdue tasks, sprints to complete, health checks due, risk follow-ups, unhealthy dependencies, dependencies at risk, unassigned requests, tasks due this week, requests assigned to you, unread notifications. Where you placed the widgets makes no difference to which counts show.

A count you never act on — unread notifications, if you read them from the bell — can be switched off in the [Customize panel](#switching-counts-off), and its place goes to the next one.

| Count | What it counts | Where it goes |
|-------|----------------|---------------|
| Unread notifications | Records with changes you have not read, the same count as the bell; shown as 99+ above 99 | Your [notifications](./notifications.mdx) |
| Overdue tasks (assigned to you) | Open tasks and milestones assigned to you on approved and active projects whose due date has passed | The My project tasks widget below |
| Due this week (assigned to you) | Open tasks and milestones assigned to you on those projects due by the end of this week (Saturday) | The My project tasks widget below |
| Health checks due (on projects you lead) | Active projects you own or manage with no current health check: none was reported, or the last one expired | The Project health checks due widget below |
| Risk follow-ups | Open risks assigned to you whose follow-up date is today or past | The My risks widget below |
| Dependencies at risk (on your teams) | Open [dependencies](./work-management/work-items.mdx#work-item-dependencies) of the teams My teams shows that are at risk (neither end planned) or unhealthy (planned to finish after they are needed); a dependency between two of them counts once | The My teams widget below |
| Sprints to complete (in your teams of teams) | Sprints that a team in one of the teams of teams My teams of teams shows started and that are past their end without being completed | The My teams of teams widget below |
| Unhealthy dependencies (across your teams of teams) | Open cross-team dependencies of the teams of teams My teams of teams shows that are unhealthy; a dependency two of them share counts once | The My teams of teams widget below |
| Unassigned requests (on your desks) | Open [intake requests](./demand-management/index.mdx) on the desks Desk queue shows (of those you are a Desk Admin or Agent on) that no one is assigned to | Requests, filtered to those desks and Unassigned |
| Requests assigned (to you) | Open intake requests assigned to you on the desks Desk queue shows | Requests, filtered to Assigned to me and, when Desk queue shows only some of your desks, to those |

These counts follow the [teams, teams of teams and desks](#teams-and-desks) their widgets show, and Desk queue's sections: with its desks left out there are no unassigned requests, and with **Assigned to me** left out no requests assigned. A count shows only when its widget does: without permission to view projects there are no task or health check counts, with no project task assigned there are no task counts, unless you own or manage a project there is no health check count, without the Following flag there is no unread count, without permission to view risks there are no risk follow-ups, without permission to view work items there are no dependencies at risk or unhealthy dependencies, without permission to view iterations there are no sprints to complete, unless you are a member of a team of teams there are no sprints to complete or unhealthy dependencies, and without the Demand Management flag there are no request counts. A count that fails to load keeps its place and shows a dash rather than a zero.

## Widgets

This is each widget's usual place. Until you [customize](#customizing) the page, Wayd reorders them around your work, as [How the default is chosen](#how-the-default-is-chosen) describes. Where a widget says how many it lists, how far ahead or back it looks, or which records it counts, that is where it starts: its [settings](#widget-settings) change it.

### Main column

- **What's changed** — your five latest [notifications](./notifications.mdx): what changed, on which record, and why you were told. Opening one goes to the record and marks it read. **View all** opens the full list.
- **My projects** — how many approved and active projects you hold a role on, broken down by role (Sponsor, Owner, PM, Member, Task Assignee). **View all** opens the Projects Dashboard.
- **My work items** — the backlog-level [work items](./work-management/work-items.mdx) assigned to you, across every workspace, that are in progress or proposed: in progress first, then proposed, each in backlog order. Epics, features and tasks are left out. It lists up to eight and says how many more there are. Each shows its status, its type and its sprint (or, outside a sprint, its team), and opens the work item. Below them is how many you finished in the last seven days, today included, when you finished any.
- **My project tasks** — the open project plan tasks and milestones assigned to you on approved and active projects that are overdue or due by the end of next week: overdue first, then by due date. It lists up to five, and says how many more are due in that time and how many open tasks are due later or have no due date; when nothing is due by the end of next week, it says so and how many are due later. Each shows its due date and its project; the task opens the project's plan. A task's due date is its planned end, a milestone's its planned date. Only tasks assigned to you are listed: owning or managing a project does not add its tasks. Shown only once you have a project task assigned.
- **My risks** — the open [risks](./planning/risks.mdx) assigned to you, with their exposure and follow-up date, soonest follow-up first. A follow-up that is today or past is highlighted.
- **Desk queue** — what waits on the [intake desks](./demand-management/index.mdx) you are a Desk Admin or Agent on. Each desk shows your role and its open requests, with how many no one is assigned to and, on a desk that scores or estimates, how many have no score from the desk's model or no estimate. Below the desks are the open requests **Assigned to me** and those you are **Participating** in, five of each, with a link to the rest of those assigned to you. Being the Demand Management Admin does not put every desk here: only the desks you are a member of. **View all** opens Requests filtered to your desks.

### Side column

- **Planning intervals** — the [planning intervals](./planning/planning-intervals.mdx) running now and those starting in the next two weeks, with their dates, how many days are left (today included) or until they start, and links to Plan Review and the current iteration.
- **My teams** — a section for each active team you are a member of, built for how that team works today: the methodology of the operating model in effect on today's date in the team's own time zone. The team's name links to its page.
  - A **Scrum** team running a sprint shows its sprint: health, predictability, velocity, work in progress, say/do and the burn-up. Between sprints it says so; the sprint appears while it runs.
  - A **Kanban** team shows its flow: the items in progress, the items finished over the last 14 days (or since the team became Kanban, if later) with their median cycle time, its current reporting period when it has a [cadence](./organizations/index.mdx#operating-model), and the item that has been in progress longest, with how many days.
  - Every team shows its open dependencies, with how many are at risk or unhealthy, and its open risks, each linking to that section of the team page. The dependency count includes dependencies within the team, so it matches the team page.
  - A team with no operating model shows only its dependencies and risks.

  - A team in a team of teams says which one it is **Part of**, linking to it.

  Each part needs the permission the team page needs for it: viewing iterations for the sprint, viewing work items for flow and dependencies, and viewing risks for risks. **View all** opens the Teams list. If you are on no team — including when you belong only to a team of teams — it says so.
- **My teams of teams** — a card for each team of teams you are a member of directly — as a release train engineer, product manager or architect, say. Being on one of its teams does not make you a member of it, so that only gets you the team's own section in My teams. Shown only to members of a team of teams. **View all** opens the Teams list.
  - The header gives its name, linking to its page, how many teams (and teams of teams) are in it today and their methodologies ("6 teams · 4 Scrum, 2 Kanban"), and the [planning interval](./planning/planning-intervals.mdx) running now that it or one of its teams takes part in, with how many open objectives of theirs have a current health check of At Risk or Unhealthy.
  - Each team or team of teams in it today has a row: a status, its name linking to its page, **Your team** when you are on it, and one signal. Teams needing attention come first, then those to watch; those on track are collapsed into **+ N on track**, which shows them until you choose **Hide on track**. Hover a row's status dot to read its status.
    - A **Scrum** team shows its running sprint with the share done against the share expected done by now, the same comparison as the sprint's health: its commitment against its burn-down once the commitment point has passed and its history is read in full, and otherwise its work against the share of its days gone. On track within 10 points, watch up to 25 points behind, needs attention beyond. Between sprints the row says **No active sprint**. A sprint the team started that is past its end and not completed shows as **ended, not completed** and needs attention; the sprint links to its page, where it can be completed.
    - A **Kanban** team shows its items in progress and how old the oldest is. It needs watching when the oldest has been in progress more than twice the team's median cycle time over the last 14 days (counted as at least a day), and attention past three times. A team that finished nothing in those 14 days has no median to compare with and shows as on track.
    - A team with no operating model shows only its name.
    - A team of teams inside it shows how many teams are beneath it, at any depth, and how many need attention or watching, and takes the worst status among them. The card goes one level down: its teams are not rows of their own.
  - The footer gives its open cross-team dependencies — those of every team beneath it whose predecessor is not done, as its Dependencies section lists them — with how many are at risk or unhealthy, and its own open risks with those of the teams beneath it. Each links to that section of the team of teams page.

  The card's parts need the same permissions as the team of teams page's: viewing iterations for sprints, viewing work items for flow and dependencies, viewing risks for risks, and viewing planning intervals for the planning interval, with viewing planning interval objectives for its at-risk count. Without permission to view a team's sprint or flow, its row shows only its name and counts as on track.
- **Project health checks due** — the active projects you own or manage whose [health check](./ppm/projects.mdx#project-health-checks) needs reporting: those with none current first (including one whose check expired while the page was open), then those whose current check expires within seven days, soonest first, with how many days are left. It lists up to five and says how many more there are. When every check lasts longer, it says so. Shown only to project owners and managers; an owner or manager whose projects are all still approved sees a line saying so.
- **Deliverables I own** — the open [deliverables](./ppm/projects.mdx#project-deliverables) you are the owner of on any project, whether or not you hold a role on it, that are past their target date or due within the next 14 days: past target first, then by target date. It lists up to five, and says how many more are due in that time and how many you own that are due later or have no target date. Each shows its status, target date and project, and opens the project's deliverables.
- **My requests** — the intake requests you asked for or submitted for someone else that are open, or that closed in the last 30 days: open ones first, each with its status, its desk, what it became and, once an outcome reports it, how far its [delivery](./demand-management/index.mdx#delivery) has got (not shown for a request that was declined, withdrawn or marked a duplicate). A project shows how many of the deliverables mapped to it are accepted, or **Met** once all are; a work item shows its status. **View all** opens My Requests, which lists all of them.
- **My roadmaps** — the active [roadmaps](./planning/roadmaps.mdx) you are an owner of, earliest start first. Each shows its dates and visibility and opens the roadmap; one whose end date has passed is highlighted, since it needs extending or archiving. It lists up to five and says how many more you own; **View all** opens the Roadmaps list. Archived roadmaps, and roadmaps you can see but do not own, are not listed. Needs permission to view roadmaps, and shown only to roadmap owners.
- **Following** — the records you [follow](./following.mdx), most recently changed first. It lists up to eight and says how many more you follow; **View all** opens your Following list.

## How the default is chosen

The columns above are the starting point. Until you customize the page, Wayd reorders them around what you are involved in, so you land on your own work without setting anything up. It works this out from your records each time you open the page — your team memberships, the roles you hold and tasks assigned to you on approved and active projects, the risks assigned to you, the intake desks you work, the open requests you raised and the active roadmaps you own — and never asks or stores what your job is.

- **What you lead or do every day comes first.** If you sponsor, own or manage a project, My projects and Deliverables I own move to the top of the main column, and if you own or manage one, so does Project health checks due. If you have a project task assigned, My project tasks moves up too. If you are a member of a team, My teams moves to the top of the main column too, followed by your My work items; if you are a member of a team of teams, so does My teams of teams. If you work an intake desk, Desk queue moves up as well.
- **Areas you work in come next.** My projects and Deliverables I own for a project member or task assignee, My risks when risks are assigned to you, My requests when you have an open request, My roadmaps when you own an active roadmap, and Planning intervals when you are on a team or a team of teams.
- **Shared widgets follow** — What's changed and Following, and My work items for someone who is not a member of a team (being on a team of teams alone does not count), since Wayd does not count your work items to decide.
- **Areas you are not part of go last.** They still show, with a line saying how they fill.

Someone involved in several areas gets them interleaved in that order; widgets that rank the same keep their usual order. The **Needs attention** counts do not follow this order: they always go most urgent first.

Once you save a layout it wins: involvement no longer moves anything you placed. A widget that becomes available to you afterwards still joins where the default would put it, and **Reset to default** returns to the involvement-based order.

## Customizing

The gear (**Customize home**), at the top right beside the greeting, opens a panel listing every widget you can see, under **Main** and **Side**. There you can:

- **Reorder** a widget by dragging its handle up or down.
- **Move** a widget to the other column by dragging it there.
- **Switch a widget off** with its switch, and back on the same way. A widget you switch off keeps its place in the list, so it returns where it was.
- **Change what a widget shows** by opening its row with the arrow beside its menu. See [Widget settings](#widget-settings).
- **Switch a count off** under **Choose counts to show**. See [Switching counts off](#switching-counts-off).
- **Reset to default** to put every widget back where it starts, switch them all on, clear every widget's settings and switch every count back on.

Each widget also has a menu (**⋯**) with **Move up**, **Move down** and **Move to side column** (or **main column**), which does the same without dragging — from the keyboard, or on a phone. With the keyboard you can also drag: focus a handle, press Space to pick the widget up, the arrow keys to move it, and Space again to drop it.

The page behind the panel changes as you go. Your layout is saved when you close the panel, with **Done** or the close button; if it cannot be saved, a message says so and the page goes back to the layout you last saved. The layout is saved to your account, not the browser, so it follows you to every device you sign in on.

The **Needs attention** row always stays at the top of the page. Its counts follow the widgets they come from — switch off My project tasks and its overdue and due-this-week counts leave the row — but not their order: the row always shows the most urgent counts above zero, up to four.

### Switching counts off

Below the columns, the panel's **Choose counts to show** section lists the counts your widgets offer, most urgent first, each with a switch. Switch one off and it never shows in the row, even when it is above zero; its place goes to the next count by urgency. You choose which counts show, not their order.

A count whose widget is switched off cannot show either way, so its switch is greyed out with a note naming the widget. It keeps whether you switched it off, so it comes back as you left it when you switch the widget on again. Counts you switch off are saved with your layout, and switching one off changes nothing else about the layout.

What the panel offers follows what you can see. A widget you gain access to after customizing (say a feature flag is turned on) appears at its usual position. One you lose access to disappears from the page and the panel, but your layout remembers it and its settings, so it comes back as you left it if your access returns.

### Widget settings

Every widget lets you change something about what it shows. Each has a gear at the top right of its card, which appears when you point at the card or move the keyboard focus into it and is always shown on a touch screen. The same settings are in the Customize panel: open the widget's row with the arrow beside its menu.

- **Rows to show** — how many the widget lists: 3, 5, 8, 10 or 15. What's changed, My work items, My project tasks, Project health checks due, Deliverables I own, My requests, My roadmaps and Following have it. Desk queue has **Requests per list**, which sets both its Assigned to me and Participating lists. A widget still says how many more there are, and **View all** still opens the full list.
- **When there is nothing to show** — what a widget does when it has nothing to list. Every widget has it.
  - **Explain** (the default) — the line saying how the widget fills.
  - **One line** — the title with a short "nothing" beside it, such as *No open risks*.
  - **Hide** — the widget shows while it loads, then leaves the page. A line at the foot of the page names every widget hidden this way ("2 widgets have nothing to show: My risks and My requests."), and its **Show** brings them back until you leave the page. When every widget is hidden, that line is the page. A widget whose data could not be loaded is never hidden: it always says so. Hiding a widget does not remove its counts from the row across the top, and selecting one of those counts brings the hidden widgets back.

  A widget has nothing to show when you have none of what it lists. My project tasks and Deliverables I own have something while you have any open, even if none is due soon. My work items has nothing with none in progress or proposed (or in progress, when it leaves proposed out), even if it says how many you finished.

Some widgets also let you choose how far ahead or back they look and which records they take in. Each starts where the widget's description above says, and its wording follows what you pick ("due in the next 30 days"):

| Widget | Setting | Choices | Starts at |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What's changed | **Unread only** | on or off | off |
| My projects | **Statuses counted** | any of Proposed, Approved, Active, Completed and Canceled | Approved and Active |
| My work items | **Include proposed items** | on or off | on |
| My work items | **Count work finished in the last** | 7, 14 or 30 days | 7 days |
| My project tasks | **List tasks up to** | **Overdue**; **Due this week**, adding those due by Saturday; **Upcoming**, adding those due by the end of next week | Upcoming |
| My project tasks | **Include milestones** | on or off | on |
| Project health checks due | **List checks expiring within** | 7 or 14 days | 7 days |
| Deliverables I own | **List deliverables due within** | 14, 30 or 60 days | 14 days |
| Planning intervals | **List intervals starting within** | 14 or 30 days | 14 days |
| My requests | **Keep closed requests for** | 7, 30 or 90 days | 30 days |
| My requests | **Open only** | on or off | off |
| Following | **Record types** | any of Project, Program, PI Objective, Request and Incident | all of them |

#### Teams and desks

If you are on several teams or desks but follow only some day to day, My teams, My teams of teams and Desk queue let you choose which show:

| Widget | Setting | Choices | Starts at |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| My teams | **Teams** | any of the teams you are a member of | all of them |
| My teams | **Sprint card** | **With burn-up**, or **Figures only**, leaving out the burn-up chart | With burn-up |
| My teams of teams | **Teams of teams** | any of the teams of teams you are a member of directly | all of them |
| Desk queue | **Desks** | any of the desks you are a Desk Admin or Agent on | all of them |
| Desk queue | **Sections** | any of **My desks**, **Assigned to me** and **Participating** | all of them |

While every team or desk is checked, one you join later shows too. Once you uncheck one, only those you checked show: the settings say how many are not shown, and a team or desk you join later is not shown until you check it. Checking them all again goes back to showing every one, including later ones. A team or desk you leave drops out of the list. If that leaves every remaining one checked, the settings say that any you join later will still not be shown and offer **Show any you join later too**; if you have left all of those you chose, the widget shows every one again. A choice holds up to about 55 teams or desks; with more than that checked, check them all instead. Unlike the other settings, these also narrow the widget's [counts](#needs-attention), and **View all** on Desk queue opens Requests filtered to the desks it shows.

A setting that picks several, such as **Statuses counted**, always keeps at least one. These settings change only the widget: the counts across the top keep their own meaning, so **Due this week** still counts the tasks due by Saturday and **Overdue tasks** includes milestones, whatever My project tasks lists. **View all** opens the full list, unfiltered. Desk queue has nothing when you are on no desk, or when every request list it shows is empty; showing only **My desks**, it has something while you are on a desk.

A change made from a card applies and saves at once. A change made in the panel shows on the page behind it and saves with the rest of the layout when you close the panel. A widget whose settings differ from where they start is marked **Customized** in the panel, and its settings have a **Reset to default** of their own, which puts that widget back without touching the others.

Settings are saved to your account with your layout. Changing a setting from a card does not fix the page's order: if you have not arranged the page, the other widgets go on following [what you are involved in](#how-the-default-is-chosen).
