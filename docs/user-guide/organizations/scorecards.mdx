# Scorecards and KPIs

A **KPI** (key performance indicator) says what a team, a project, a program or a portfolio is aiming for and keeps what its number was at each point in time: where it started, where it is trying to get to, and a measurement for each period since. A record's **scorecard** is every active KPI on it, grouped by category.

KPIs need the `kpis` feature flag, which an administrator turns on under **Settings → System → Feature Flags**. With it on, every team and team of teams has a **Scorecard** section, right after Overview, as does every [program and portfolio](../ppm/portfolios-programs.mdx); a [project](../ppm/projects.mdx) has it after Deliverables.

## What a KPI holds

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Name and description** | What is measured and why it matters. |
| **Category** | The group the scorecard shows it under, such as Flow, Quality or People. Categories are the organization's own list (see [Categories](#categories)). A KPI may have none; uncategorized KPIs are listed last. |
| **Prefix and suffix** | A symbol before the value (`$`) and a unit after it (`days`, `%`, `items`). |
| **Direction** | Whether higher or lower is better. |
| **Baseline** | Where the KPI started, and optionally the dates that value was taken over — "an average of 20 days over the last quarter". A KPI can start without one and have it added later. |
| **Target** | Either **a value** to reach ("reach 15 days"), or **a percentage change** from the baseline in the KPI's direction ("improve 25%"). A percentage change needs a baseline. The baseline must be short of the target: a KPI that should go up starts below its target. |
| **At-risk threshold** | Optional. A line short of the target, stated as a value or as a share of the way from baseline to target. A measurement past it but short of the target is **At Risk** rather than **Unhealthy**. Without one, a KPI is either on target or not. |
| **Show as** | Which reading the scorecard leads with: the value, the change from baseline, or the progress to target. |
| **Measurement window** | What span of time each value covers — per period, cumulative, rolling or point in time. See [Measurement windows](#measurement-windows). Fixed once the KPI has measurements. |
| **Rolling average** | Optional, for a per-period KPI. Reads the KPI as the average of its latest 2 to 12 measurements instead of the latest alone — velocity over the last 7 sprints, say. |
| **Checkpoint plan** | Optional. Dated checkpoints the KPI is expected to reach on the way to its target. See [Checkpoint plans](#checkpoint-plans). |

A percentage target follows its baseline: correct the baseline and the target it resolves to moves with it.

## Measurements

A **measurement** is the raw value of the KPI for the span of time its [measurement window](#measurement-windows) says. Every measurement keeps the days it covers, so the latest value and the trend are never ambiguous.

The value is always the raw number, never a percentage worked out from it. Record 14 for a cycle time of 14 days; the change from baseline and the progress to target are worked out from it, so nobody enters a figure Wayd cannot check.

A measurement can also carry a **sample size** — how many items an average or a rate was worked out from — and a note.

### Measurement windows

A KPI's window decides what span each value covers, and so how you say when a value was taken:

| Window | Each value covers | You record it | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Per period** | Its own period: a sprint, a week, a month. Periods never overlap. | For a period | Values that belong to one period: velocity per sprint, throughput per week. |
| **Cumulative** | Every day from a fixed **anchor date** to the day it is taken. | As of a day | A number since a start date: "cycle time since February 16, 10% better than last year", recorded every Friday. |
| **Rolling** | A fixed number of days ending on the day it is taken. | As of a day | A number over a moving window: "cycle time over the last 90 days". |
| **Point in time** | The state at one moment. | At a moment | Counts that describe a moment, not a span: open bugs, work in progress. Several a day are fine. |

A cumulative or rolling KPI takes one value per day; Wayd works out the days it covers. A point-in-time reading keeps its exact moment, shown in your own time zone, and counts toward the day it falls on in the organization's [system time zone](../settings/index.mdx#general).

The window is chosen when the KPI is added and cannot change once it has measurements: they were recorded under it. To measure differently, close the KPI and add a new one.

### Rolling averages

A per-period KPI whose values add up per period can be read as the **average of its latest measurements**, 2 to 12 of them. A team's velocity, recorded each sprint, read over its last 7 sprints, is the usual case. The average is what the target, health and trend judge; the card's headline says it is an average, and the KPI's details also show the latest single value. While a KPI has fewer measurements than the average asks for, it averages what it has and says how many.

The average is worked out when read, never stored, so changing how many it covers reads through the whole history at once.

## Checkpoint plans

A KPI aiming at a value a year away would read as **Unhealthy** for most of that year if every measurement were held to the final target. A **checkpoint plan** says where the KPI is expected to be along the way, so it is judged against the plan instead.

Each checkpoint has a **date**, a short **label** such as `Q1` or `Sprint 12`, a **planned value**, and optionally an **at-risk value**. One checkpoint per date.

- **A measurement is read against the first checkpoint on or after the last day it covers** — the end of its period, the day it is as of, or the day of its moment for a point-in-time KPI. A value for March is judged against the Q1 checkpoint dated March 31; a value for April against Q2.
- **Health uses that checkpoint's lines:** **Healthy** at or past its planned value, **At Risk** past its at-risk value, **Unhealthy** otherwise. A checkpoint with no at-risk value is either on plan or not — the KPI's own at-risk threshold is not used inside the plan, because it is set against the final target.
- **After the last checkpoint**, the KPI's target and at-risk threshold apply as they do without a plan.
- **Change from baseline and progress to target** are still worked out toward the final target. A rolling average is judged against the checkpoint of its newest measurement.

An at-risk value must be short of its planned value in the KPI's direction — below it for a KPI that should go up. Planned values need not climb steadily: a seasonal plan may dip. The last checkpoint need not equal the target. Reversing a KPI's direction is refused while its plan holds at-risk values, since they would then sit on the wrong side: remove them, change the direction, then add them back.

On a KPI's view, **Checkpoint Plan** edits the plan as a whole: add, change and remove rows, then save. The chart draws the plan, and its at-risk values, as stepped lines beside the measurements, and the plan's table shows each checkpoint with the measurement read against it and how it fared. The scorecard card shows the checkpoint the latest value was read against beside the target. A closed KPI's plan cannot change.

## Reading the scorecard

Each KPI is a card showing:

- **Its headline** — the latest value (or rolling average), its change from baseline, or its progress to target, as the KPI is set to show.
- **Health** — **Healthy** once the target is met, **At Risk** past the at-risk threshold, **Unhealthy** otherwise; with a [checkpoint plan](#checkpoint-plans), against the checkpoint the latest value falls under. Nothing until the KPI is first measured.
- **Trend** — whether the latest value moved the right way from the one before it. For a KPI read as a rolling average, whether the average moved the right way from the average one measurement earlier.
- **A line of its recent values**, and the baseline and target.

Open a card for the KPI's full view: its goal, every reading, a chart of its history against the target, at-risk and baseline lines, and every measurement. **Show closed KPIs** lists the KPIs taken off the scorecard.

## Changing KPIs

From the scorecard, **Add KPI** adds one at the end. From a KPI's view:

- **Record Measurement** adds a value — for a period, as of a day, or at a moment, as the KPI's window takes it. **Correct** and **Remove** in a measurement's row change one already recorded; who took it stays as recorded.
- **Checkpoint Plan** replaces its plan; see [Checkpoint plans](#checkpoint-plans).
- **Edit** changes its details, baseline and target in one save, so a baseline and target can move together. Each part that changed is its own entry in the KPI's history.
- **Close** takes it off the scorecard and keeps its measurements; a closed KPI takes no new measurements until it is **reopened**.
- **Remove** deletes it and its measurements for good. Close it instead to keep its history.

Every change is recorded in the KPI's history and in the team's, project's, program's or portfolio's **Activity**.

### Who can change a team's KPIs

Members of the team, in any role, and members of **any** team of teams above it in the hierarchy as it stands today. A team of teams' own KPIs are changed by its members and those of the teams of teams above it. Viewing a team's KPIs needs only permission to view teams.

The **Administer KPIs** permission changes any record's KPIs and manages the categories. See [KPIs: who may change them](../settings/permissions.mdx#kpis-who-may-change-them).

### Who can change a project's, program's or portfolio's KPIs

Whoever may change the record itself: the record's **Update** permission together with [delivery leadership](../settings/permissions.mdx#delivery-leadership-in-ppm) — Owner or Manager on the record, or on its program or portfolio. A project's KPIs can therefore be set by its portfolio's Owner without a role on the project. Sponsors and project Members cannot change them. The [PPM Administrator](../settings/permissions.mdx#ppm-administrator) permission stands in for the role, never for the Update permission.

A completed or canceled record's KPIs stay open to change, so the benefits a project was meant to bring can be measured after it closes.

Viewing a record's KPIs needs only permission to view that kind of record.

## Categories

**Settings → Organization → KPI Categories** holds the organization's categories, in the order every scorecard groups by. Drag a row to reorder. A category can be renamed, and deactivated to stop it being offered for new KPIs — KPIs that have it keep it. A category no KPI uses, open or closed, can be deleted. Managing categories needs **Administer KPIs**.

## Bringing in history

A year or more of history per team, project, program or portfolio, from a spreadsheet, is loaded with the [KPI import](./bulk-import.mdx#kpi-import): one file of KPIs, one of their measurements, and optionally one of their checkpoint plans.

## From an AI assistant

The `kpis` toolset on the [MCP server](../../getting-started/mcp-server.mdx) does what the scorecard does: it reads a scorecard and a KPI's history, records, corrects and removes measurements, reads and replaces checkpoint plans, and adds, edits, closes, reopens, reorders and removes KPIs, under the same rules for who may change them.
