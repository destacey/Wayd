# Permissions

Wayd answers "may this user do this?" with **two independent checks**. Most of the application uses only the first; Project Portfolio Management and the sprint lifecycle use both.

1. **Permission claims** — granted through [roles](./index.mdx#roles). These decide whether a user can reach a feature at all.
2. **Membership of the record** — whether the user belongs to the record being changed. In PPM that is delivery leadership: a permission claim alone is not enough to change a project, program, or portfolio. For a sprint it is [team membership](#sprint-lifecycle-team-membership).

Both must pass. Holding `Permissions.Projects.Update` lets you reach the project edit endpoints; being a project Owner decides *which* projects you may change.

## Permission claims

Permissions are named `Permissions.{Resource}.{Action}` and follow a consistent pattern:

| Action | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `View` | Read entities |
| `Create` | Create new entities |
| `Update` | Modify existing entities |
| `Delete` | Remove entities |
| `Import` | Bulk-load entities from a file |
| `Run` | Trigger an operation — for example running or requeuing a [background job](./index.mdx#background-jobs) |
| `Manage` | Create, update and delete in one permission — used for planning interval objectives |
| `Search` / `Export` | Search or export users |
| `Review` | Review records for a review group, only where you belong to it (see [Intake review](#intake-review-desk-membership)) |
| `Administer` | Domain-wide administrative standing (see [PPM Administrator](#ppm-administrator)) |

Some resources add specific actions — `Permissions.Projects.ManageProjectWorkItems` and `Permissions.Teams.ManageTeamMemberships`.

The server checks a user's permissions against their current roles on every request, so a role or permission change applies to what they can do straight away. The menus and buttons the app shows are decided by the permissions carried in the user's sign-in token, so they catch up on the user's next token refresh.

## Delivery leadership in PPM

Viewing PPM records needs only the `View` permission. **Changing** one additionally requires that you lead it.

You lead a record if you hold the **Owner** or **Manager** role on it, or on anything above it:

| Record | You may manage it if you are Owner or Manager of… |
|---|---|
| Project | the project, **or** its program, **or** its portfolio |
| Program | the program, **or** its portfolio |
| Portfolio | the portfolio |

Leadership inherits **downward**: a portfolio Owner leads every program and project inside it, without needing a role on each one.

:::note[Sponsors are deliberately excluded]
Sponsors fund and oversee work; they don't run delivery. A Sponsor — at any level — cannot change status, edit details, or assign roles. Neither can a project **Member**, who contributes to the work without leading it.
:::

### What the rule covers

Every operation that changes a project, program, or portfolio:

- **Status transitions** — Approve, Activate, Pause, Resume, Complete, Cancel, Close, Archive
- **Editing details** — name, description, business case, expected benefits, expenditure category
- **Editing the timeline** — start and end dates
- **Assigning roles** — adding, removing, or replacing role assignments
- **Project structure** — changing a project's key, program, or lifecycle
- **Strategic themes** — changing the themes a project or program supports
- **Delivery signals** — recording health checks and scores
- **Scoring** — assigning or clearing a portfolio's scoring model
- **[Ranking](../ppm/portfolios-programs.mdx#portfolio-project-ranking)** — reordering a portfolio's projects

Role assignment is included for a reason: it is the path by which leadership itself is granted. If it were not gated, anyone holding the `Update` permission could make themselves an Owner and then do everything else.

### What it does not cover

- **Viewing** anything — the `View` permission alone is sufficient.
- **Creating** portfolios, programs, and projects — governed by the `Create` permission. A newly created record has no leadership yet.
- **Deleting** — governed by the `Delete` permission.
- **[Bulk imports](../ppm/bulk-import.mdx)** — governed by the relevant `Import` permission. Import is an administrative bulk operation, and the records it creates have no leadership at the moment it runs.

## PPM Administrator

`Permissions.ProjectPortfolioManagement.Administer` grants **domain-wide delivery-leadership standing across PPM**. A holder is treated as leading every project, program, and portfolio.

It substitutes for *membership only*. It does **not** substitute for the permission claim — a PPM administrator still needs `Permissions.Projects.Update` to change a project. The two checks stay independent.

Grant it to people who administer delivery without sitting in the hierarchy: PMO staff, platform administrators, and anyone who needs to set up or repair portfolios they don't personally lead.

:::tip[It is required to seed a new portfolio]
A portfolio has nothing above it to inherit leadership from, so a brand-new portfolio has no one who can assign its first Owner. A PPM administrator is the only one who can — after which normal leadership takes over.
:::

The seeded **Admin** role includes this permission.

## Sprint lifecycle: team membership

[Starting, completing and reopening a sprint](../work-management/sprints.mdx#starting-completing-and-reopening-a-sprint), [correcting its actual dates](../work-management/sprints.mdx#correcting-actual-dates), and changing its [team days off](../work-management/sprints.mdx#team-days-off) or [sprint type](../work-management/sprints.mdx#sprint-type), needs `Permissions.Iterations.Update` **and** membership of the sprint's team, in any role. Members of the team of teams the team belongs to on the day also qualify — only its direct team of teams, not the ones above it.

`Permissions.Iterations.Administer` waives the membership for every team's sprints, the way the [PPM Administrator](#ppm-administrator) permission waives leadership. Like it, it never substitutes for the `Update` permission. The seeded **Admin** role includes both.

Viewing sprints needs only `Permissions.Iterations.View`.

## Intake review: desk membership

Reviewing an [intake request](../demand-management/index.mdx) — changing its status, assigning its reviewer, moving it to another desk, recording what it became, applying tags, scoring it, and reading or writing internal notes — needs `Permissions.IntakeRequests.Review` **and** being a reviewer on the request's desk. Desk reviewers are a list named on each desk, never derived from teams or departments.

`Permissions.IntakeRequests.Administer` waives the desk membership, and also manages desks and channels. It never substitutes for the `Review` permission. The seeded **Admin** role includes both.

Being a reviewer on any desk lets you read the full detail of requests on every desk, because a reviewer cannot judge whether a request belongs on another desk without reading it. Most people need only `View` and `Create`: a requester can edit their own request while it is still proposed without `Update`. What `View` reveals depends on the desk's [visibility](../demand-management/index.mdx#who-can-see-a-request).

## Allocations: team membership

Changing [how much of a person's time a team has](../organizations/index.mdx#allocations) needs `Permissions.Teams.Update` **and** membership of that team, in any role. Members of the team of teams the team belongs to on the day also qualify — only its direct team of teams, not the ones above it. A change that touches several teams, such as moving a person's time from one team to another, needs this for each of them.

`Permissions.Teams.Administer` waives the membership for every team's allocations. It never substitutes for the `Update` permission. The seeded **Admin** role includes both.

Viewing a person's allocations needs only `Permissions.Employees.View`.

## Time away: the person and their managers

Recording, changing and removing a person's [time away](../organizations/index.mdx#time-away-and-holiday-calendars) needs **no permission**. It is allowed for:

- The person, for their own time away
- Anyone the person reports up to: their manager, their manager's manager, and so on to the top
- Anyone holding `Permissions.EmployeeTimeAway.Administer`

The first two come from who you are, so your user account must be linked to an employee. The `Administer` permission covers everyone else: people with no manager in Wayd, and time away recorded on someone's behalf. The seeded **Admin** role includes it.

The same people are the only ones shown the **reason** for a stretch of time away. Everyone who can view the employee (`Permissions.Employees.View`) sees the days and how much of each, and so does everyone who can see a team's capacity.

Setting the holiday calendar a person takes, or their [working day](../organizations/index.mdx#working-day), is not time away: each needs `Permissions.Employees.Update`. The person and their managers cannot set either without it.

## Why an action might be unavailable

When a PPM or sprint action is missing from a menu or rejected on submit, it is one of:

| Cause | What to do |
|---|---|
| Missing the permission claim | Ask an administrator to grant it via **Settings → Access → Roles** |
| Not leading the record | Ask an Owner or Manager of the record — or of its program or portfolio — to assign you a role, or to perform the action |
| Not on the sprint's team | Ask to be added to the team or its team of teams, or ask a member to perform the action |
| Not the person or one of their managers | A person's time away is recorded by them or by someone they report up to. Ask one of them, or a time away administrator |
| No linked employee record | PPM and sprint changes are attributed to an employee. Ask an administrator to link your user account to one. |
| A domain rule blocks it | The action may be invalid for the record's current state — for example, a portfolio cannot close while it still contains open projects |

## Upgrading to this version

**PPM changes are membership-gated after upgrading.** Before this version, any user holding a PPM `Update` permission could change any project, program, or portfolio — including assigning roles on it.

After upgrading, users who hold the permission but lead nothing will find PPM edit and status actions unavailable. This is deliberate.

To restore access for someone who needs it, either:

- Assign them an **Owner** or **Manager** role on the relevant records — or on a portfolio or program above them, which covers everything beneath; or
- Grant them **`Permissions.ProjectPortfolioManagement.Administer`** if they legitimately administer delivery across the domain.

Members of the seeded **Admin** role are unaffected — it includes the administrator permission.
