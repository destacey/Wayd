# Permissions

Wayd answers "may this user do this?" with **two independent checks**. Most of the application uses only the first; Project Portfolio Management and the sprint lifecycle use both.

1. **Permission claims** — granted through [roles](./index.mdx#roles). These decide whether a user can reach a feature at all.
2. **Membership of the record** — whether the user belongs to the record being changed. In PPM that is delivery leadership: a permission claim alone is not enough to change a project, program, or portfolio. For a sprint it is [team membership](#sprint-lifecycle-team-membership).

Both must pass. Holding `Permissions.Projects.Update` lets you reach the project edit endpoints; being a project Owner decides *which* projects you may change.

## Permission claims

Permissions are named `Permissions.{Resource}.{Action}` and follow a consistent pattern:

| Action | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `View` | Read entities |
| `Create` | Create new entities |
| `Update` | Modify existing entities |
| `Delete` | Remove entities |
| `Import` | Bulk-load entities from a file |
| `Run` | Trigger an operation — for example running or requeuing a [background job](./index.mdx#background-jobs) |
| `Manage` | Create, update and delete in one permission — used for roles, privileged users and planning interval objectives |
| `Administer` | Domain-wide administrative standing (see [PPM Administrator](#ppm-administrator), [Planning Poker](#planning-poker-facilitators-and-invites), [Story Maps](#story-maps-owners-and-editors) and [Roadmaps](#roadmaps-owners)) |

Some resources add specific actions — `Permissions.Projects.ManageProjectWorkItems`, `Permissions.Teams.ManageTeamMemberships` and `Permissions.RateLimits.Elevated`.

The server checks a user's permissions against their current roles on every request, so a role or permission change applies to what they can do straight away. That includes live updates: someone with a planning poker session or story map open stops receiving its changes as soon as they lose its `View` permission or their account is deactivated, or, for a private poker session or story map, as soon as it stops admitting them. The one exception is [`Permissions.RateLimits.Elevated`](#elevated-rate-limits), which by default takes up to five minutes. The menus and buttons the app shows are decided by the permissions carried in the user's sign-in token, so they catch up on the user's next token refresh.

## Delivery leadership in PPM

Viewing PPM records needs only the `View` permission. **Changing** one additionally requires that you lead it.

You lead a record if you hold the **Owner** or **Manager** role on it, or on anything above it:

| Record | You may manage it if you are Owner or Manager of… |
|---|---|
| Project | the project, **or** its program, **or** its portfolio |
| Program | the program, **or** its portfolio |
| Portfolio | the portfolio |

Leadership inherits **downward**: a portfolio Owner leads every program and project inside it, without needing a role on each one.

:::note[Sponsors are deliberately excluded]
Sponsors fund and oversee work; they don't run delivery. A Sponsor — at any level — cannot change status, edit details, or assign roles. Neither can a project **Member**, who contributes to the work without leading it.
:::

### What the rule covers

Every operation that changes a project, program, or portfolio:

- **Status transitions** — Approve, Activate, Pause, Resume, Complete, Cancel, Close, Archive
- **Editing details** — name, description, business case, expected benefits, expenditure category
- **Editing the timeline** — start and end dates
- **Assigning roles** — adding, removing, or replacing role assignments
- **Project structure** — changing a project's key, program, or lifecycle
- **Strategic themes** — changing the themes a project or program supports
- **Delivery signals** — recording health checks and scores
- **Scoring** — assigning or clearing a portfolio's scoring model
- **[Ranking](../ppm/portfolios-programs.mdx#portfolio-project-ranking)** — reordering a portfolio's projects
- **[KPIs](../organizations/scorecards.mdx)** — adding, changing and removing a record's KPIs, their measurements and checkpoint plans

Role assignment is included for a reason: it is the path by which leadership itself is granted. If it were not gated, anyone holding the `Update` permission could make themselves an Owner and then do everything else.

### What it does not cover

- **Viewing** anything — the `View` permission alone is sufficient.
- **Creating** portfolios, programs, and projects — governed by the `Create` permission. A newly created record has no leadership yet.
- **Deleting** — governed by the `Delete` permission.
- **[Bulk imports](../ppm/bulk-import.mdx)** — governed by the relevant `Import` permission. Import is an administrative bulk operation, and the records it creates have no leadership at the moment it runs.

## PPM Administrator

`Permissions.ProjectPortfolioManagement.Administer` grants **domain-wide delivery-leadership standing across PPM**. A holder is treated as leading every project, program, and portfolio.

It substitutes for *membership only*. It does **not** substitute for the permission claim — a PPM administrator still needs `Permissions.Projects.Update` to change a project. The two checks stay independent.

Grant it to people who administer delivery without sitting in the hierarchy: PMO staff, platform administrators, and anyone who needs to set up or repair portfolios they don't personally lead.

:::tip[It is required to seed a new portfolio]
A portfolio has nothing above it to inherit leadership from, so a brand-new portfolio has no one who can assign its first Owner. A PPM administrator is the only one who can — after which normal leadership takes over.
:::

The seeded **Admin** role includes this permission.

## Sprint lifecycle: team membership

[Starting, completing and reopening a sprint](../work-management/sprints.mdx#starting-completing-and-reopening-a-sprint), [correcting its actual dates](../work-management/sprints.mdx#correcting-actual-dates), and changing its [team days off](../work-management/sprints.mdx#team-days-off) or [sprint type](../work-management/sprints.mdx#sprint-type), needs `Permissions.Iterations.Update` **and** membership of the sprint's team, in any role. Members of the team of teams the team belongs to on the day also qualify — only its direct team of teams, not the ones above it.

`Permissions.Iterations.Administer` waives the membership for every team's sprints, the way the [PPM Administrator](#ppm-administrator) permission waives leadership. Like it, it never substitutes for the `Update` permission. The seeded **Admin** role includes both.

Viewing sprints needs only `Permissions.Iterations.View`.

## Planning Poker: facilitators and invites

A [planning poker session](../planning/planning-poker.mdx#poker-sessions) is run by its facilitators, and a private one admits only the people it names, so poker has three permissions.

| Who | What they can do | How they get it |
|---|---|---|
| `Permissions.PokerSessions.View` | See public sessions and the private ones they are admitted to; join them, and vote or turn voting off | A role |
| `Permissions.PokerSessions.Create` | Create a session, becoming its first facilitator | A role |
| A **facilitator** of a session | Run it — rounds, reveals, re-votes and accepted estimates — edit it, change its facilitators, visibility and invites, complete it and delete it | Named on the session, with `View` |
| `Permissions.PokerSessions.Administer` | What a facilitator can do, in every session, private ones included | A role |

A private session admits its facilitators, the employees invited to it, and the members of the teams invited to it, including members of an invited team's current team of teams. An invite never replaces the `View` permission. Every poker action but reading needs an account linked to an employee, because facilitators, invitees and members are employees.

Live updates follow the same rule: changing a session's visibility or invites disconnects anyone it no longer admits. Someone who leaves an invited team keeps their open connection until they next open the session.

The seeded **Admin** role includes `Administer`.

## Story Maps: owners and editors

A [story map](../planning/story-maps.mdx#who-can-see-and-edit-a-map) is managed by its owners, and its visibility and edit access decide who else can see and change it, so Story Maps has three permissions.

| Who | What they can do | How they get it |
|---|---|---|
| `Permissions.StoryMaps.View` | See public maps and the private ones they are on. Change the content of a public map open to anyone who can view it | A role |
| `Permissions.StoryMaps.Create` | Create a map, becoming its first owner | A role |
| An **editor** of a map | Change its content — goals, steps, tasks, swim lanes, personas, checklists and work item links — and see it when it is private | Named on the map, or a member of a team named on it, with `View` |
| An **owner** of a map | What an editor can do, and edit its name and description, archive and delete it, and change its owners, editors, visibility and who can edit | Named on the map, with `View` |
| `Permissions.StoryMaps.Administer` | What an owner can do, on every map, private ones included | A role |

A team named as an editor brings in its current members and the members of its current team of teams. Being named on a map never replaces the `View` permission. Every story map change needs an account linked to an employee, because owners and editors are employees.

Live updates follow the same rule: changing a map's visibility, owners or editors disconnects anyone it no longer admits. Someone who leaves an editor team keeps their open connection until they next open the map.

The seeded **Admin** role includes `Administer`.

## Roadmaps: owners

A [roadmap](../planning/roadmaps.mdx) is changed only by its owners, so Roadmaps has three permissions.

| Who | What they can do | How they get it |
|---|---|---|
| `Permissions.Roadmaps.View` | See public roadmaps and the private ones they own | A role |
| `Permissions.Roadmaps.Create` | Create or copy a roadmap, becoming one of its owners | A role |
| An **owner** of a roadmap | Edit its details, owners and visibility, configure its colors, archive, activate and delete it, and add, change and delete its items | Named on the roadmap, with `View` |
| `Permissions.Roadmaps.Administer` | What an owner can do, on every roadmap, private ones included | A role |

An owner must keep themselves among the owners when editing a roadmap; an administrator need not be an owner at all. Being named on a roadmap never replaces the `View` permission. Every roadmap change needs an account linked to an employee, because owners are employees.

The seeded **Admin** role includes `Administer`.

## Demand Management: desk roles

[Demand Management](../demand-management/index.mdx) works differently from the rest of Wayd: a permission says what you can do anywhere, a role on a desk says what you can do on that desk, and no action needs both. Most of what happens to a request is decided by desk roles, so Demand Management has only two permissions.

| Who | What they can do | How they get it |
|---|---|---|
| Anyone signed in with a linked employee record | Submit a request; read, edit (while it is proposed) and reply to their own requests and the requests they participate in; withdraw a request they asked for while it is open | Nothing to grant |
| `Permissions.IntakeRequests.View` | Browse other people's requests, as far as each desk's [visibility](../demand-management/index.mdx#who-can-see-a-request) allows | A role |
| **Agent** on a desk | Act on that desk's requests: read them in full, edit, change their status, assign, move to another desk, manage participants, and — as later features arrive — score and keep internal notes | Named on the desk |
| **Desk Admin** on a desk | Everything an agent does, plus run the desk — its details, settings, review workflow and agents — and delete its requests | Named on the desk by the Demand Management Admin |
| `Permissions.IntakeRequests.Administer` (the Demand Management Admin) | Create and delete desks, change a desk's visibility, name Desk Admins, manage channels, and act as Desk Admin on every desk | A role |

Desk members are a list named on each desk, never derived from teams or departments, and someone whose employee record is inactive is a member of no desk. Working one desk gives you nothing on another: an agent reads the full detail of other desks' requests only where that desk's visibility allows it for everyone. The seeded **Admin** role holds both permissions.

## Allocations: team membership

Changing [how much of a person's time a team has](../organizations/index.mdx#allocations) needs `Permissions.Teams.Update` **and** membership of that team, in any role. Members of the team of teams the team belongs to on the day also qualify — only its direct team of teams, not the ones above it. A change that touches several teams, such as moving a person's time from one team to another, needs this for each of them.

`Permissions.Teams.Administer` waives the membership for every team's allocations. It never substitutes for the `Update` permission. The seeded **Admin** role includes both.

Viewing a person's allocations needs only `Permissions.Employees.View`.

## KPIs: who may change them

Adding, changing and removing a team's KPIs and their measurements needs membership of that team, in any role, or of **any** team of teams above it as the hierarchy stands today — not only its direct team of teams, so a leader can set KPIs anywhere in their part of the organization. The same goes for a team of teams' own KPIs. No permission claim is needed.

Reading a team's KPIs needs only `Permissions.Teams.View`.

A project's, program's or portfolio's KPIs follow the [delivery leadership](#delivery-leadership-in-ppm) rule, as every other change to the record does: its `Update` permission (`Projects`, `Programs` or `ProjectPortfolios`) and Owner or Manager on the record or an ancestor, with `Permissions.ProjectPortfolioManagement.Administer` standing in for the role. Reading them needs the record's `View` permission. They stay changeable after the record is completed or canceled.

`Permissions.Kpis.Administer` waives the membership, and for a PPM record the `Update` permission and leadership too, for every record's KPIs and manages the organization's KPI categories. The seeded **Admin** role includes it. A read-only token changes nothing, whatever the person holds.

## Links: the record they are on

The web links kept on a record have no permissions of their own. Whoever can see the record sees its links, and whoever can edit the record can add, change and remove them:

| Links on | Changing them needs |
|---|---|
| A team or team of teams | `Permissions.Teams.Update` |
| A planning interval | `Permissions.PlanningIntervals.Update` |
| A planning interval objective | `Permissions.PlanningIntervalObjectives.Manage` |
| A risk | `Permissions.Risks.Update` |
| A project, program or portfolio | Its `Update` permission **and** [delivery leadership](#delivery-leadership-in-ppm) of it. An archived portfolio's links cannot be changed |
| A strategic initiative | `Permissions.StrategicInitiatives.Update` |
| An incident | `Permissions.Incidents.Update`, with an account linked to an employee |
| A sprint | `Permissions.Iterations.Update` **and** [membership of its team or its team of teams](#sprint-lifecycle-team-membership); `Permissions.Iterations.Administer` waives the membership, never the `Update` permission. A sprint whose team is not mapped to a Wayd team takes no changes, as with every other sprint change |

A read-only token changes nothing, whatever the person holds. Deleting a record removes its links.

## Time away: the person and their managers

Recording, changing and removing a person's [time away](../organizations/index.mdx#time-away-and-holiday-calendars) needs **no permission**. It is allowed for:

- The person, for their own time away
- Anyone the person reports up to: their manager, their manager's manager, and so on to the top
- Anyone holding `Permissions.EmployeeTimeAway.Administer`

The first two come from who you are, so your user account must be linked to an employee. The `Administer` permission covers everyone else: people with no manager in Wayd, and time away recorded on someone's behalf. The seeded **Admin** role includes it.

The same people are the only ones shown the **reason** for a stretch of time away. Everyone who can view the employee (`Permissions.Employees.View`) sees the days and how much of each, and so does everyone who can see a team's capacity.

Setting the holiday calendar a person takes, or their [working day](../organizations/index.mdx#working-day), is not time away: each needs `Permissions.Employees.Update`. The person and their managers cannot set either without it.

## Elevated rate limits

Every signed-in user has a [request budget](../../reference/api.mdx#rate-limits) shared by their session, personal access tokens and MCP connections. `Permissions.RateLimits.Elevated` gives a user a larger one: by default five times the standard budget, but never unlimited.

Grant it to accounts whose steady traffic outgrows what a person needs — a service account that syncs data, or a script or agent that makes many calls — by adding it to a role in **Settings → Access → Roles** and giving that role to the account. The account does not need to be an admin. The seeded **Admin** role includes it.

By default a grant or revoke takes up to five minutes to apply, because Wayd remembers each user's budget for that long rather than checking it on every request.

## User administration and privileged permissions

User administration can be delegated. Someone who manages users can onboard, support and offboard the people they look after, and every change they make is recorded in the user's **Activity**. Inside that, they can act as any ordinary user: whoever can reset someone's password can sign in as them, as on any help desk. Grant the user-management permissions to people you trust with that.

**Privileged permissions change other people's access.** An account holding one is a *privileged account*, and user administration does not reach it without `Permissions.PrivilegedUsers.Manage`:

| Permission | Why it is privileged |
|---|---|
| `Permissions.Roles.Manage` | Creates, renames and deletes roles and chooses what they grant |
| `Permissions.OidcProviders.Create` / `.Update` / `.Delete` | Decide who can sign in, and which role new sign-ins are given |
| `Permissions.PrivilegedUsers.Manage` | Reaches privileged accounts, and grants the roles that make them |

The **Roles** page tags these permissions **Privileged**.

**Privileged accounts are out of a user administrator's reach.** Without `Permissions.PrivilegedUsers.Manage`, you cannot make any change to a privileged account: its details, email or linked employee, its password, how it signs in, whether it is active or locked out, or its roles. The account's roles count even while it is inactive. The **Admin** role holds every permission, so every Admin is a privileged account.

**Privileged roles are granted only with `Permissions.PrivilegedUsers.Manage`.** Assigning a role that carries a privileged permission, whether when creating a user or afterwards, is refused without it. Every other role can be assigned by anyone with `Permissions.Users.Create` (at creation) or `Permissions.UserRoles.Update`, including roles granting permissions you do not hold yourself.

**Nobody changes their own roles or linked employee.** Another administrator makes those changes, so taking on access always involves a second person. A linked employee carries its PPM leadership, manager chain and team memberships.

**Wayd keeps at least one administrator.** An administrator here is an active user whose roles grant every permission, whether through **Admin** or a role of your own. A change that would leave none is refused: deactivating them, removing their roles (from the user or from the role's members), or removing a permission from the role that makes them one. People sync leaves that account active, and sign-in never locks it out while no other administrator could unlock it.

A role's permissions must be ones Wayd defines; saving a role with a permission name Wayd does not recognise is refused.

## Why an action might be unavailable

When a PPM or sprint action is missing from a menu or rejected on submit, it is one of:

| Cause | What to do |
|---|---|
| Missing the permission claim | Ask an administrator to grant it via **Settings → Access → Roles** |
| Not leading the record | Ask an Owner or Manager of the record — or of its program or portfolio — to assign you a role, or to perform the action |
| Not on the sprint's team | Ask to be added to the team or its team of teams, or ask a member to perform the action |
| Not the person or one of their managers | A person's time away is recorded by them or by someone they report up to. Ask one of them, or a time away administrator |
| No linked employee record | PPM and sprint changes are attributed to an employee. Ask an administrator to link your user account to one. |
| The user is privileged | Changing a user who holds a privileged permission, or assigning a role that grants one, needs `Permissions.PrivilegedUsers.Manage`. See [User administration](#user-administration-and-privileged-permissions) |
| It is your own account | Nobody changes their own roles or linked employee. Ask another administrator |
| A domain rule blocks it | The action may be invalid for the record's current state — for example, a portfolio cannot close while it still contains open projects |

## Upgrading to this version

**Links follow the record they are on.** `Permissions.Links.View`, `.Create`, `.Update` and `.Delete` are removed, and the upgrade deletes them from every role; roles that held them are given nothing in their place. A record's links are now seen by whoever can see the record and changed by whoever can edit it — see [Links](#links-the-record-they-are-on). Someone who could change links everywhere may find they no longer can on a project, program or portfolio they do not lead, or a sprint whose team they are not on. The upgrade also removes links whose record has since been deleted. A link address saved with no scheme, such as `example.com/page`, is completed with `https://`, which is how it was already opened; an address saved with another scheme, such as `ftp://`, is left as it was and still opens. New and changed addresses must start with `http://` or `https://`.

**Role management is one permission.** `Permissions.Roles.Create`, `.Update` and `.Delete` and `Permissions.RoleClaims.Update` are replaced by `Permissions.Roles.Manage`, and `Permissions.RoleClaims.View` by `Permissions.Roles.View`. The upgrade gives `Roles.Manage` only to roles that held `RoleClaims.Update`, which could already choose what roles grant; roles that held only `Roles.Create`, `.Update` or `.Delete` lose them, since alone they could not change what any role grants. `Permissions.Users.Search`, `.Delete` and `.Export` are removed: no endpoint ever checked them. To let someone manage roles again, grant them `Permissions.Roles.Manage`.

**Privileged accounts need a new permission.** Changing a privileged account, or assigning a role that grants a privileged permission, needs `Permissions.PrivilegedUsers.Manage` (see [User administration](#user-administration-and-privileged-permissions)). The seeded **Admin** role holds it; anyone else who looks after administrators' accounts needs it added to a role.

**PPM changes are membership-gated after upgrading.** Before this version, any user holding a PPM `Update` permission could change any project, program, or portfolio — including assigning roles on it.

After upgrading, users who hold the permission but lead nothing will find PPM edit and status actions unavailable. This is deliberate.

To restore access for someone who needs it, either:

- Assign them an **Owner** or **Manager** role on the relevant records — or on a portfolio or program above them, which covers everything beneath; or
- Grant them **`Permissions.ProjectPortfolioManagement.Administer`** if they legitimately administer delivery across the domain.

Members of the seeded **Admin** role are unaffected — it includes the administrator permission.

**Story maps are managed by their owners after upgrading.** `Permissions.StoryMaps.Update` and `Permissions.StoryMaps.Delete` are removed, and the upgrade deletes them from every role; roles that held them are given nothing in their place. Every existing map becomes public and editable by anyone who can view it, so anyone with `View` can still change its content. Each map's owner becomes its only owner when their account is linked to an employee; a map whose owner had no linked employee has no owner, and only a holder of `Permissions.StoryMaps.Administer` can archive, delete or share it, or name a new owner.

**Roadmap managers are now called owners.** `Permissions.Roadmaps.Update` and `Permissions.Roadmaps.Delete` are removed, and the upgrade deletes them from every role; roles that held them are given nothing in their place. Every roadmap keeps its managers as its owners, and owners can change their roadmaps with `View` alone. To let someone manage roadmaps they do not own, grant `Permissions.Roadmaps.Administer`.
